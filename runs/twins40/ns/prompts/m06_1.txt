The attached images are real cards from the game. Draw one new card that could sit beside them unnoticed: the same rendering, lighting, finish, color and level of detail.
Draw the object turned about 20-30 degrees into a three-quarter view, even when it is symmetric; only a face meant to be read, such as a clock or a sign, faces the camera. Keep its true proportions and mild perspective, from a low camera, so openings and rims are thin ovals. It spans about four fifths of the card. Forms are smooth, with small soft bevels. Rims, handles, cords, bands and posts stay slim, and small parts stay small.
One soft light from one side turns the form smoothly into shade.
Each material looks like itself by its color and sheen, in natural local color. Large surfaces stay smooth, with no grain, weave, nap or pores: detail lives in the parts. Shiny materials carry one soft highlight. Cloth, cord and wood stay matte.
Draw only what the subject names. No text, letters, numbers, frame, border or watermark.
Subject: A stack of three folded paper treasure maps with green land, a winding yellow dashed trail, blue lakes, and a big red X marking the spot.
Hero colors: green, yellow, red, light blue, white.
Staging: The object floats in the air, usually slightly tilted, large on the card; a big object may run past the card's edge. The background is flat and graphic, or the sky or water the object flies or swims in.
Backdrop: tone-on-tone wide stripes in hot pink, light pink.
