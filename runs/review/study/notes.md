# What the specialists see in the real cards

## shape_proportions

Draw the object at true proportions, simplified. Rigid things (boxes, books, booths, boards, panels) have flat faces, straight parallel edges and tight small corner bevels. Keep chains, hoses, ribbons, legs and rails thin and present. Only inherently soft items (pillows, gloves, shoes) are plump. Include every expected part; the outline stays clean and closed.

- Always: One solid object (or tight cluster) fills most of the card, drawn as a single readable silhouette with no cut-off except the bar scene.
- Always: Hard-surface objects (ticket, phone booth, planter, book, pump) keep straight edges and parallel sides; ticket edges, booth walls, book spine and planter rails run dead straight.
- Always: Corners are small, tight rounds or bevels, not blobs: ticket corners, book cover corners, planter posts, booth base slab.
- Always: Thin parts are kept thin and present: carousel chains, pump hose, planter legs and shelf board, book bookmark ribbons, cocktail glass stems, straw.
- Always: Soft goods (neck pillow, boxing gloves, sneakers, fins) are plump, rounded, overlapping masses, with smooth outlines and few straight runs.
- Always: Expected parts are all there: swing seats and chains, hose and nozzle on pump, door panes in booth, trunk and fronds on palm.
- Always: Silhouette is clean and closed with no fuzz; leaf and fruit edges are the only jagged contours (palm fronds, pineapple crown).
- Varies: Straightness: high on ticket, booth, planter, book; low on pillow, gloves, fins, palm.
- Varies: Corner tightness: near-square on book/planter/booth; generous rounded on ticket (still moderate) and pump dome cap.
- Varies: Thin share: very high on carousel, planter, bar stools and glasses; near zero on ticket, pillow, gloves, booth.
- Varies: Solidity: solid blob on ticket, booth, book, gloves; holes and gaps on pillow, carousel, planter, bar stools.
- Varies: Proportions: the pump has an oversized domed top and fat body, slightly toy-like; the other hard objects are near true proportion. Gloves and pillow are inflated, as the real objects are.
- Varies: Tilt: ticket and book are rotated diagonally; booth, pump, carousel and planter stand upright.
- Never: Hard boxes swollen into pillows: booth, book, planter and ticket faces are never puffy.
- Never: Thin chains, hose, legs or ribbons thickened into sausages or dropped.
- Never: Rounded blob corners on rigid panels, boards or plates.
- Never: Missing signature parts (door, hose, chains, seats, trunk).
- Never: Ragged, melted or fuzzy outlines (aside from foliage and the soft shadow bleed on the bar card).

## viewpoint_depth

Single hero object in soft 3D render, seen from slightly above in a mild three-quarter view with gentle perspective, no wide-angle distortion. Hero stands on a simple ground plane with a soft contact shadow, in front of a flat or simplified backdrop. Rounded forms are smooth and swollen, hard forms have flat faces. Background is simpler, not blurred.

- Always: Hero is a single 3D-rendered object shown from slightly above, at a mild high three-quarter angle: top face visible, one or two side faces turned toward camera, never a pure side elevation (palm, pump, booth and carousel come closest).
- Always: Perspective is mild and consistent within an object: edges converge gently (phone booth, planter, pump, book), with no fisheye or wide-angle stretching.
- Always: Hero and ground plane agree: where a floor is present, normals show a flat green-cyan plane with a horizon band behind it, and the hero base sits on it with a contact shadow or footprint.
- Always: Rounded hero forms read as smooth swollen normal gradients (pillow, gloves, fins, shoes); hard-edged forms read as even flat patches with crisp edges (booth, planter, ticket, book cover).
- Always: Depth is shallow and layered in simple tiers: hero, a ground or table, then a simple backdrop. The hero is always the nearest and largest object.
- Varies: Camera height: top-down or near top-down for flat items (ticket, pillow, fins, book on table) versus a low eye-level three-quarter for tall items (phone booth, carousel, pump, palm, planter).
- Varies: Ground presence: no ground, only an abstract flat backdrop with stripes or rings (ticket, pillow, fins); a floor with a horizon line (booth, carousel, shoes, gloves, palm, pump); a tabletop that fills the frame (book).
- Varies: Background treatment: flat graphic color or pattern (most cards), a simple painted scene (fence, desert mesas, sky), or a fully built environment with mid-ground props (the pool bar, which has foreground and background layers and a cropped roof).
- Varies: Number of layers: one (ticket) up to four or five (pool bar: roof, bar, stools, water, pool edge).
- Varies: Crop: some heroes sit fully inside the frame with margin; the gloves and the pool bar crop at the edges.
- Varies: Planar share: very high on the ticket (almost fully flat normals), moderate on the booth, planter and book, low on the pillow, gloves and fins.
- Never: Strong wide-angle distortion or forced extreme perspective.
- Never: A true bird's-eye view that makes a standing object look flat, or a worm's-eye view from below.
- Never: Heavy depth-of-field blur: backgrounds are simplified or flat, not optically blurred (a faint softening in the desert and fence scenes is the most there is).
- Never: A hero floating without any relation to its ground when a floor is shown.
- Never: Parts of one object drawn from different viewpoints, except the flat ticket, which is a graphic.
- Never: Several competing focal depths: the hero stays nearest and sharpest.

## lighting_shading

Soft clay-like 3D toy lighting from upper left. Smooth gradients across rounded forms, gentle mid-tone shadow side, no black core shadows. Broad soft sheen on lit tops, no sharp white hot spots. Soft low-contrast cast shadow on the ground to lower left, with slight contact darkening at the base. Thin lighter edge only on lit contours.

- Always: Light comes from the upper left or upper front. Tops and left faces are lightest, right and underside faces are darker. Examples: the phone booth's left face against its right, the pump's dome top, the book cover.
- Always: Form turns smoothly, with soft gradients over rounded volumes (pillow, gloves, palm trunk, stools). Hard edges only show where the geometry has a real corner, such as the booth frame, book spine and fence slats.
- Always: Shadow side stays gentle. Even the darkest areas are mid-tone and keep local color. The estimated shading reads as soft clay or matte plastic with a narrow value range.
- Always: Highlights are broad, soft sheens on the lit side, such as the glove tops, fin edges and pillow crown. They are never tiny sharp white hot spots.
- Always: Each hero has a soft, fairly low-contrast cast shadow on the ground, falling to the lower left or toward the viewer (sneakers, carousel, book, gloves). Contact darkening sits at the base or under the object.
- Always: Thin lighter edge on lit contours and a slightly darker underside give a gentle volume lift. Fine detail (booth panes, chains, lace) gets a small darkening in the gaps.
- Varies: Cast shadow presence: strong and clear for grounded objects on a floor (sneakers, carousel, book on wood, gloves). Weak or missing for the floating ticket, pillow and fins, which sit on abstract backgrounds.
- Varies: Form contrast: highest on big rounded volumes with deep folds (neck pillow wrinkles, gloves, thatch roof). Lowest on flat graphic items (ticket, fin blades, pump panel), which barely model.
- Varies: Shading range: the pillow and gloves run from near-white to a fairly deep mid-dark. The ticket and carousel sit in a compressed pale band.
- Varies: Light bias strength: moderate for the booth and pump, where one side is clearly darker. Nearly even for the ticket and carousel.
- Varies: Highlight size: wide on glossy rubber (gloves, pillow) and small rim sparkles on fins and palm leaves.
- Varies: Background lighting: the sunset pump scene has warm light and a strong gradient, while pool and garden scenes are evenly lit.
- Never: Deep black core shadows or harsh high-contrast chiaroscuro.
- Never: Sharp white specular hot spots or mirror-like chrome glints.
- Never: Hard-edged, dark, graphic drop shadows (not seen beyond a soft floor shadow).
- Never: Strong colored rim light or neon glow around the hero edges.
- Never: Visible banding or cel-shaded steps in the form turn.
- Never: Light direction that flips between objects on a card.

## value_contrast

Bright, saturated card with light-to-mid backgrounds and no heavy dark areas. Hero has smooth glossy shading with soft highlights, plus small dark accents only in gaps, openings and under-shadows. Separate hero from background by hue as well as value. Faint low-contrast rings or stripes behind it, soft slightly darker corners, no black outlines.

- Always: Overall key is bright and saturated: backgrounds sit in light or middle grey, almost never dark.
- Always: Darkest accents are small and local: gaps under the hero, cast shadows, openings, inner-ring holes, seat undersides, hose, and glove cuffs. They stay under about 10-15% of the card.
- Always: The hero is separated by hue and saturation as well as value. In grey, the hero often merges with the background into the same middle grey (book, boxing gloves, pump, planter, sneakers' upper).
- Always: Light values cluster on the hero's lit faces and highlights (fin blades, ticket face, awning stripes, sneaker soles, pump cap band).
- Always: Hero interiors are smooth, with soft glossy highlights and a few clear steps. Only small details (chain links, lace, window grid, thatch) are hard-edged.
- Always: The ground or floor is a separate, usually lighter band than the back wall (sand, shelf floor, pale yellow floor, tile, wood table).
- Varies: Background key: dark and flat behind the fins (hero lighter than background), light behind the sneakers and ticket (hero darker), mid grey behind the phone booth, carousel and gloves.
- Varies: Background structure: concentric rings (neck pillow, fins, palm), vertical stripes (ticket, gloves), or a flat gradient. Rings and stripes are very low contrast and show as a faint 1-2 grey step.
- Varies: Vignette: soft darker corners and an edge falloff on the ticket, neck pillow, fins and carousel. The booth and sneakers have a lighter spot or flat field, and the scene cards (pool bar, planter, pump) have none.
- Varies: Hero-to-background gap is large for the fins, ticket and pillow (hero face near white or near black against mid grey). It is small for the book, gloves, pump and planter, where the hero is in the same grey group.
- Varies: Scene cards (pool bar, planter, pump) fill the whole frame with five grey groups and busy mid-value texture. Single-object cards have two or three large groups.
- Varies: Glow behind the hero: a pale halo behind the phone booth and a lighter patch behind the pillow. There is none behind the others.
- Never: Pure black backgrounds or a heavy dark vignette that crushes the edges.
- Never: A dark hero silhouette on a dark field, or a hero lost against a background of equal hue.
- Never: Hard black outlines drawn around the hero. Edges come from value steps and rim highlights.
- Never: Dramatic spot lighting or a big black shadow mass covering a large part of the card.
- Never: Gritty, noisy or grainy texture inside the hero's shaded areas.

## color_palette

Hero in clear, saturated local colour (blue, red, yellow, teal) on a different-hue, equally saturated background: purple, pink, orange, green or blue, often with rings or stripes. Include only small cream, white or grey neutrals. Shadows are deeper, richer versions of the object's own hue, never black or grey. Use 2-4 hue families and keep warm and cool both present.

- Always: Hero is a saturated, clearly named local color (blue, red, yellow, teal, orange-red) set against a different-hue, equally saturated background; hero and background are about equally loud, with almost no neutral rest area.
- Always: Hero and background sit in separate hue families, usually near-complementary pairs: blue on orange or pink, yellow on purple, red on green, red on blue, teal on orange, magenta on sand.
- Always: Backgrounds are flat or gently graded fields of one hue (purple, pink, orange, green, blue) with concentric rings, stripes or a faint icon pattern. They are not neutral.
- Always: Neutrals appear only in small amounts: cream or white panels and soles, grey stone, rocks, cap rims. Large muted areas are rare.
- Always: In the 8-colour lens, shadows keep their hue family and turn darker and slightly more saturated: deeper blue on blue, maroon on red, plum on purple, dark teal on teal. Where the floor is warm, cast shadows go brown, mauve or pink.
- Always: Each card shows 2 to 4 main hue families, plus tiny accents such as lace, flags and pins.
- Varies: Warm share: ticket and boxing-glove cards are roughly 60-80% warm. Pillow, fins and phone booth are about 30-50%. The garden card is mixed and the pump card is very warm.
- Varies: Chroma: a few cards sit slightly lower, namely the shoes' pale yellow floor, the palm's sand and the pump's dusty sunset sky. Most cards are near full saturation.
- Varies: Background type: some are abstract fields (ticket, pillow, fins, carousel). Others are real scenes with natural muted tones (wood, sand, stone, sky, pool).
- Varies: Palette count is lowest on the pillow, fins and gloves, with 2 to 3 families. It is highest on the shoes, journal, bar and garden, with 5 or more.
- Varies: Shadow quality: the soft pink and mauve shadows on pale floors and the dark brown shadows under wood are quite different from the saturated blue-on-blue shading on abstract-background cards.
- Never: A desaturated, grey or sepia hero.
- Never: A hero that matches its background hue.
- Never: Black or neutral-grey shadows; shadows always carry hue.
- Never: A single-hue monochrome card.
- Never: A whole card pulled to one warm cast with no cool counterpart.
- Never: A muted, low-chroma background framing a loud hero.

## surface_finish

Smooth soft toy-plastic and clay surfaces with airbrushed gradients, no noise or brush strokes. Soft broad blurry highlights, rare small crisp glints only on glass or gauges. Surface marks are few, large and bold: chunky wood streaks, big chain links, simple ribs. No fine stitching, pores, scratches or realistic metal reflections.

- Always: Every material is rendered as smooth, soft, rounded 3D-toy plastic or clay: surfaces are airbrushed gradients with no visible brush strokes, noise, pores or scratches.
- Always: Highlights are soft, broad and blurry-edged, never hard specular dots; shadows are soft colored gradients. Rim lights and soft bounce edges are present on forms.
- Always: Texture is separate from the surface: a few big bold hand-placed marks (wood plank lines, chain links, pineapple diamonds, glove creases, pillow ribs) sit on a smooth base instead of a continuous fine grain.
- Always: Detail is simplified to chunky readable shapes; small parts (stitches, rivets, screws, buttons) are either absent or turned into large toy-like dots and bumps.
- Always: Color inside a surface shifts smoothly (hue shift in shadow, lighter warm edge), not by tiny texture variation.
- Varies: Gloss: boxing gloves, fins, phone booth, watering can and sneaker sole read semi-gloss with soft sheen; the pillow, pump body and book cover are near-matte; glass, drink glasses and gauge faces get the only crisp glints.
- Varies: Micro-texture strength: the pillow has faint fabric weave, the sneaker has a perforated mesh dot pattern, wood has drawn streaks, and the flat ticket and sticker icons have none. Others are fully smooth.
- Varies: Wood grain is large and decorative: orange-stripe streaks on the table and book background, chunky carved ring segments on the palm trunk, thin plank lines on the red bench. Scale is bold, not fine.
- Varies: Metals (chains, trowel, gauge bezel, pump nozzle) are painted as flat gray or blue-gray with a soft light edge, not real chrome reflections.
- Varies: Flat vector-style cards (ticket) have zero material texture; 3D cards have soft volumetric shading.
- Never: No hard specular glints on large surfaces, no mirror chrome or environment reflections.
- Never: No fine fabric weave, leather pores, stitching or rivet detail at small scale.
- Never: No scratches, dirt, wear, fingerprints or edge chipping, except the pump base's stylized gray patches.
- Never: No visible brush strokes, painterly texture, grain noise or photo realism.
- Never: No matte-and-shiny mix within one object that goes beyond a soft glow on the same plastic.

## edges_linework

No outlines or glow. Hero has a crisp, clean silhouette edge made only by color contrast with the background, slightly firmer on the shadowed side. Panel seams and details are thin, precise hairlines. Smooth soft volumes stay mostly edge-free. Background is either plain and soft or detailed with finer, lower-contrast lines than the hero's contour.

- Always: No drawn dark outline: the contour in the edge map comes only from the color/value step between the hero and its background. The outer silhouette is crisp and closed, one thin precise line, a little heavier on the shadow-side rim or underside (pillow, ticket base, glove).
- Always: Internal seams and panel gaps are thin, fine, single lines (phone booth window bars, ticket panels, sneaker panels, pump dial bezel). Rounded rims show as paired double lines.
- Always: The hero is clearly cut out from the background with no halo or glow around it. Edges and contrast are strongest at the hero's outer boundary and at its small high-contrast details.
- Always: Soft rounded volumes (pillow, gloves, fins) have mostly faint, smooth interior edge lines. Only the silhouette and the few creases or seams stand out.
- Varies: Edge density in the hero ranges from sparse (ticket, pillow, gloves, fins, book cover) to dense (phone booth, carousel chains, sneaker laces and mesh, thatch roof, pineapple, pump).
- Varies: Background edge density: near zero for plain gradients with faint ring or stripe edges (ticket, pillow, fins, gloves). Medium for graphic motifs (suitcase pattern, wood grain). High for scene backgrounds (fence, paving, pool tiles, desert mesas), which are drawn about as finely as the hero.
- Varies: In scene cards the hero and background are drawn at similar line weight. Hierarchy then comes from the hero's slightly darker, closed outer contour and from blur or low contrast in far areas.
- Varies: Outline darkness is light to medium. It is heaviest on cards with a strong color jump, such as the pillow's right side and the red booth against green.
- Never: Thick black cartoon outlines or sticker-style borders around the hero.
- Never: Glow, halo, or white rim around the silhouette.
- Never: Thick grooves or heavy dark gaps for seams.
- Never: Blurry, smeared, or broken outer edges.
- Never: A background that is sharper or darker in edge terms than the hero's own contour.

## composition_hierarchy

One large centered 3D toy-style object filling about 80% of the square card, with a small, even margin. Either upright or tilted about 35 degrees. Saturated primary colors, with the background in a contrasting hue. Simple backdrop: flat color with soft rings, or a plain floor. Soft contact shadow. No text, no clutter, no cropping.

- Always: One single hero object (or one tight cluster) sits in the middle of the square card and fills roughly 70-90% of its width or height.
- Always: The hero is a saturated, warm or primary-colored 3D toy-like render (blue, red, yellow, orange) set against a contrasting background hue: blue on orange or pink, yellow on purple, red on green or blue, red on purple-pink.
- Always: The hero is the first read at thumbnail size, and its silhouette names the object: ticket, neck pillow, fins, phone booth, swing ride, sneakers, book, gloves, palm, pump.
- Always: The hero is lit and has a soft contact shadow or sits on a base, so it stands apart from the background.
- Always: The background is simple and behind the hero: a flat color with radial rings, stripes or a swirl, or a plain backdrop with a floor plane.
- Varies: Margins range from tight to roomy. The pillow, boxing gloves, book and ticket come within about 5-10% of the edges. The sneakers, fins and swing ride leave about 10-15%. The sneakers have empty background above them.
- Varies: Placement is either upright and centered (phone booth, swing ride, pump, planter) or tilted about 30-45 degrees (ticket, fins, book). The gloves are cropped at the left edge and the pillow is nearly cropped at the top.
- Varies: Background complexity varies. Flat gradients (ticket, pillow, fins, carousel) are quiet. Scenic backgrounds (planter with fence, pump with desert, palm with sand) are busier. The tiki bar scene fills the card edge to edge, with no single hero.
- Varies: Some hero shapes fill the card as a tall block (phone booth, pump), others as a wide shape (sneakers, fins, gloves, planter).
- Varies: Some cards add small secondary props, such as the watering can and trowel, the fruit and cocktails, or the sand rocks. They sit inside the hero's footprint and the hero still wins.
- Never: A tiny hero lost in empty space. Even the smallest hero fills more than about half the card.
- Never: An off-center hero that pushes the card into a lopsided layout.
- Never: Text, UI frames or badges competing with the hero.
- Never: A dark or muddy background, or a hero in the same hue as its background.
- Never: Several equal-weight objects spread across the card. The exception is the tiki bar scene, where a dense scene replaces the single hero.

## staging_detail

One centered hero filling most of a square card. Backdrop is a bright, smooth gradient with huge quiet shapes: soft rings, stripes, or faint tone-on-tone icons. Simple ground plane with a soft shadow. Environments, if any, are a few large cartoon shapes with at most two or three object-related props. No faces, sparkles or text.

- Always: One hero fills roughly 60-85% of the square card, centered or slightly off-center, with the background fully hidden behind it, never cropped by it.
- Always: The backdrop is a smooth saturated gradient or soft pattern in a color that contrasts with the hero (blue ticket on orange, blue pillow on pink, yellow fins on purple, red booth on green).
- Always: Backdrop shapes are very large and low-contrast: vertical stripes, concentric rings, a swirl, faint tone-on-tone suitcase icons, a plain wall and floor split.
- Always: Where there is a ground it is a simple plane (sand, cream floor, wood planks, stone paving) with a soft contact shadow under the hero.
- Always: Backdrop edge detail is low everywhere, apart from the few environment cards; the grey-out shows the background as calm, continuous fields.
- Always: Props, when present, are few and tied to the object: watering can and trowel on the planter shelf, bookmarks on the book, cocktails and fruit on the bar.
- Varies: Staging ranges from floating on a flat field (ticket, pillow, fins) to a ledge or floor with shadow (booth, carousel, sneakers) to a real material (wood table, planks) to a real scene (palm on dunes, garden with fence, desert gas pump).
- Varies: Environment load rises in the scenes: a garden fence with bushes and paving, a pool bar with a thatch roof, tiled water and stools, and desert mesas are busier, with many small repeated shapes (fence slats, thatch shingles, pool tile).
- Varies: Backdrop pattern type changes card to card: stripes, rings, spiral, icon repeat, wood grain, blue wall boards.
- Varies: Background palette is mostly warm or saturated, with sky gradients on the scene cards. Horizon height varies from none to a mid-card split.
- Varies: Hero and background are sometimes similar in hue (red booth on green is complementary; red gloves on blue wall with orange wood is complementary) and sometimes analogous in warmth (orange pump against a sunset desert).
- Never: Faces, eyes, sparkles, confetti, stars or invented whimsy decorations.
- Never: Text, logos or captions in the backdrop.
- Never: A cluttered or high-contrast backdrop pattern that fights the hero.
- Never: Dark, dramatic or photographic environments; scenes stay bright and cartoon-clean.
- Never: Background figures, people, animals or a crowd of secondary props.
- Never: A hard-edged black outline or a busy mid-ground between hero and backdrop.
