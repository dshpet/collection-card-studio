## Art director

Yes, this is close. The set reads as the same game at card size, in render, palette and backdrop types, and p11 passes as it stands. Most remaining tells are small and fixable: flat front-on light, added sparkles and glows, and grey materials without their own color. The exceptions are p12, a foil product shot that is the clearest outsider, and p04, with its glow and streaky ice; the asteroid's sparkles are the next easiest tell.

Overruled:
- All proportion, thickness, roundness, framing and camera fixes, because they are out of scope for this pass. That covers most of the shape_proportions, viewpoint_depth and composition_hierarchy reports.
- Surface_finish picks on real cards (p01, p05, p06, p08, p09, p10).
- Value_contrast picks on real cards (p05, p07, p11). These control and real-card calls show how noisy those two reports are.
- Requests to calm backgrounds in general, because real fields are just as saturated.
- Requests for less cream paper, because the real envelope and scroll are the same cream.
- Requests to ground floating objects, because real heroes float on pattern fields too.
- The contour complaint in edges_linework, which was weak on the cards. Several specialists also admitted the sci-fi subjects swayed their picks, so I discounted their low-confidence calls.

I kept the tube's straw, even though staging_detail wants it gone, because it may be part of the subject and the rendering is the real fault.

1. **Soft, front-on light: forms turn evenly like clay, with no committed shadow side. Cast shadows are weak or missing, and bright sources do not light nearby surfaces. This is the main shared tell, seen on the capsule, observatory, station, plant and rocket. On the plant and rocket it also makes the card bright and flat.** Light the hero from one side. Give it one bright side and a broad, darker, cool shadow side in middle tones. Keep pure white to a few small highlights. Anchor each cast shadow tight at the contact point and let it fade outward.
2. **Added decoration that a real card would not carry: four-point sparkles, ember dots, glow halos that spill onto the backdrop, beads on the globe ring, tiny star dots and evenly spaced grass tufts. These are the cheapest fixes and the clearest generated marks on the asteroid and comet.** Show only the object on a clean backdrop. Keep backdrop patterns crisp right up to the object's edge, and let large quiet shapes carry the setting.
3. **Neutral materials lose their own color. Grey metal picks up the backdrop, shadows go dead charcoal or turn into a saturated copy of the surface color, and the card collapses toward one hue family. Clearest on the capsule, asteroid, station and tube.** Paint metal and grey rock with cool blue-grey or slate-violet shadows. Make the deepest shadows a rich dark color, not flat charcoal, and keep the backdrop color out of the metal.
4. **Finish drifts away from the card look on single cards. The tube is a foil product photo with fine crinkles and a hard side shadow. The comet's ice has thin veins and its tail is smeared white streaks. Other cards hold the quiet matte-and-shiny mix, so this is a per-card fault.** Use smooth, soft stylized painted surfaces: one broad sheen, a few simple soft folds or facets, and no fine crinkles, veins or wispy streaks.
5. **Two backdrops run slightly hotter than the real range and compete with the hero: the comet's violet and the globe's lime. This is minor and applies to those two cards only. Real cards use loud saturated fields, so the broad request to calm backgrounds is overruled.** Deepen those two backdrops slightly and keep them saturated.

- p12: Repaint the silver tube as soft stylized satin metal with one broad vertical sheen, a few simple soft folds and a broad darker side, and reduce the cap to a few soft grooves. Use cool blue-grey shadows in the metal with no pink in them. Shorten the shadow so it stays soft and tight at the base, and deepen the pink wall slightly.
- p04: Remove the orange sparkle and the ember dots, and remove the pale glow around the rock and tail. Paint the ice as clean mid-blue facets with a broad darker blue shadow on the underside, white only in a few small highlights, and a smooth tail with no streaks. Deepen the violet backdrop slightly and keep it saturated.
- p02: Remove all the gold sparkles. Give the rock a cool slate-violet shadow side at the lower right and crisp lit crater rims at the upper left. Remove the orange glow at the edge of the rock.
- p09: Light the rocket from one side: true white with a bright strip and a broad cool blue-grey shadow side, not cream. Add dark cast shadows from the fins and tower across the pad. Remove the small grass tufts and lower the sky and grass slightly in brightness.
- p06: Paint the hull warm off-white with neutral grey shading, a darker underside and a clear curved shadow from the ring across the tube. Change the white panel frames to soft mid-grey. Remove the small star dots.
- p01: Paint the hull as cool blue-grey metal with a bright strip on the left and a darker band down the right. Make the underside a rich deep navy-violet. Add soft shadows under the panels and around the porthole rim.
- p08: Remove the round glow rings from the wall, keeping the bulbs bright. Let warm yellow bulb light fall on the nearby leaves and stem curls. Deepen the pot's shadow side and make its table shadow start tight at the base.
- p10: Remove the two small beads from the ring. Add a thin curved shadow from the ring and arm across the sphere. Slightly deepen the green, and make the gold a warmer orange-brass with amber shadows.
- p03: Add a long soft shadow across the grass from the base, and make one side of the drum and dome slightly cooler and darker. Paint the stone a paler grey-beige with lilac shadows. Remove the grass tufts.
- p11: keep
