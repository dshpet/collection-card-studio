## Art director

Close. Most generated cards already match the real staging, palette and soft toy rendering. I'd keep 4 of the 10 as they are: the opera glasses, picnic basket, bench and teapot. The main thing that gives the other cards away is fine surface texture: canvas, straw and linen weave, streaky felt, and cobblestones. Two of them have the scene problem as well: the door is shot from too far back and the pram sits on a busy floor. Lighting is a minor issue. Six cards get small edits, each touching material, light or ground only, with no change to form. Several specialist claims were overruled because the two real control cards show the same traits: the real spyglass floats centred with a glow, and the real keyboard has a backdrop of music notes and stars.

1. **Fine, crisp surface texture that the real cards don't have: canvas weave on the parasol, dense straw weave on the bonnet, linen weave on the tablecloth, streaky brushed sheen on the top-hat felt, and grids of small hard cobblestones under the pram. This is the strongest tell: real cards (fan, beach umbrella, scarf, gloves, taxi) keep surfaces smooth, matte to satin, with broad soft shading.** Per card: render the named material as smooth matte or satin with a few broad, soft bands or folds and one gentle highlight. Draw floors as a few large soft slabs or a plain surface. Do not change form.
2. **Pulled-back full-scene staging with invented extras and a busy ground: the Georgian door sits small in a big stone wall with corner bushes, paving and ivy, and the pram stands on a large cobbled floor. Real scene cards (blue door, golf club, coaster) crop close and keep a few large, quiet shapes around the hero.** Only for scene cards: bring the camera closer so the hero fills the card and is cut by the edge, and drop decorative extras. Do not apply this to floating-backdrop cards; the real spyglass and keyboard float centred with margins.
3. **Soft, front-lit heroes with little turn into a shadow side and weak contact shadows (parasol, teacup, top hat). Real cards show one key light from the upper left, a clearly darker right side and a firm contact shadow.** In notes, add a slightly darker right side and underside plus a firmer contact shadow. Use 'slightly'; don't push it into hard contrast.
4. **Hard mirror metal with white glints on the pram's chrome frame and spokes, where the real cards use satin metal (taxi bumper, golf iron).** Ask for satin silver with soft, broad highlights only where the card already shows chrome.
5. **Overruled: the shape_proportions and form-based calls (swollen barrels, thick brims, spindly pram frame). Form can't be compared across different objects. Also overruled: the calls on the controls. The real spyglass has a pale glow, a centred float, wide margins and a near-black hero on a light field, and the real keyboard has music notes and stars. So glow-behind-hero, centred floating composition and themed backdrop motifs are real traits here, not tells. Warm-on-warm color complaints are also overruled, since the real fan is violet on yellow and the real fountain is warm throughout.** Ignore these points. Don't change proportions, backdrop glows or the framing of floating cards.
