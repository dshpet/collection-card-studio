## Art director

Fairly close. Palette, rendering and backdrop types match the game, and 3 of the 9 generated cards come near passing: the yellow UFO is a keep, and the silver UFO and the helmet need only small notes. The worst giveaways are the antenna (a photographic concrete roof, colourless and pale) and the tablet (brushed metal and a flat cyan screen), then the purple rover and the colourless drone on a white-hot sunburst. Shared root causes: photographic grey materials, one hue flooding hero and backdrop, spotlight glows behind the hero, and decorative extras. Many generated heroes are also small and centred with empty margins, but that is framing and cannot be fixed with these notes. Overruled: the shape and proportion fixes (out of scope); the surface specialist on frost, condensation and shoe mesh (all on real cards); the staging and shape calls on the sandcastle's bucket and spade (real); lighting on the bumper car (real); and calls that picked a real card as the generated one: value and colour on p02 B; colour on p07 B; surface on p06 B, p10 B and p12 A; value on p10 B; lighting on p12 A.

1. **Photographic grey materials and settings: concrete rooftop with seams and grain (antenna), brushed-metal plate (tablet), streaky mirror chrome with rivets and bolted rings (rocket, UFO hull). The real cards paint every surface as broad, soft, coloured plastic or paint, even when it is glossy (airplane, vending machine, bumper car).** Paint metal as smooth satin with one broad soft highlight. Put objects on warm, simply painted surfaces such as wood, tiles or a soft gradient. Show hardware as a few wide smooth bands.
2. **One hue floods the card and neutral heroes have no local colour of their own. The rover, ground and sky are all lilac. The battery caps go pink and the helmet shell goes purple from the backdrop. The drone is white and charcoal on all-yellow, and the star chart is one flat ultramarine slab. Real cards set a clearly coloured hero against a contrasting backdrop, usually warm on cool, and their shadows stay coloured.** Give the hero its own clear local colour that contrasts with the backdrop. Shade white and steel parts with cool neutral grey, not with the background hue.
3. **A spotlight glow or near-white patch sits behind or under the hero: a white burst at the centre of the drone's sunburst, a pale floor under the battery, a light patch behind the helmet, and bulb glow spilling onto the UFO rings. Real backdrops stay one even mid-tone (the lamp's rays, the vending machine's purple).** Keep the backdrop one even mid-tone from centre to corners, with the brightest values only on the hero itself.
4. **Invented extras and decoration the subject does not need: a house badge on the helmet ear cup, rivets and screw dots, and evenly scattered pebbles and stars on the moon ground. Real cards add one meaningful partner prop (the magnifier, the duck, the bucket), not scattered decoration. Overruled: the bucket and spade, the frost, the condensation and the shoe mesh are all on real cards.** Keep surfaces plain apart from one purposeful prop, and replace decorative badges and hardware with simple flat colour shapes.
5. **Small, whole, centred hero with even empty margins (drone, battery, antenna, rover, UFOs), while real cards come close and let the edge crop the subject. This is real, but it is framing, which these notes may not change. Flagged for the style rules only.** Frame the hero close so it fills most of the card, and let an edge crop it where natural.

- p01: Sunshade: Make the saucer hull a soft satin silver with one broad highlight along the top of the rim, in place of the mirror streaks. Keep the green rings even, with no glow from the bulbs spilling onto them. Keep the red dome and the rim bulbs.
- p02: Rocket: Remove the rivets on the nose cone and the bolts around the porthole, leaving a plain thick porthole rim. Make the silver body a soft satin with one broad highlight on the left, in place of the hard chrome stripe. Add a slight warm orange glow from the flame on the nozzle and lower fins; keep the red nose, the fins and the clouds.
- p03: keep
- p04: Map: Make the chart's blue a slightly lighter, softer navy with faint teal and violet nebula patches, in place of the flat ultramarine. Draw the grid and constellation lines slightly softer and hand-painted, without glow. Keep the cream frame, the gold corners, the stars and the compass.
- p06: Power Cell: Make the metal caps a neutral steel grey with cool blue-grey shadows, in place of the pink tint. Tint the floor the same mid pink as the wall, with no pale patch at the bottom. Let the cyan core glow slightly onto the inner faces of the caps; keep the yellow ring and the glass tube.
- p08: Drone: Make the sunburst one even mid yellow-orange from centre to corners, with no white glow behind the drone. Give the drone body a soft blue shell with white trim and cool grey shadows. Make the rotors dark blue-grey with a soft light top edge; keep the camera and the arms.
- p09: Lighthouse: Replace the grey concrete roof with warm terracotta roof tiles in a few broad, soft painted shapes, with a short, soft, pale shadow. Paint the mast with red-and-white bands. Keep the red beacon, the dish, the antenna rods and the sky.
- p10: Moon Rover: Make the moon ground a cool neutral grey with a few large smooth rocks and no scattered pebbles. Make the sky a deep blue-navy in place of the purple. Shade the white rover with cool grey shadows; keep the orange box, the dish and the seats.
- p11: Travel Book: Lay the tablet on a warm orange wooden desk in place of the brushed metal. Show a colourful map picture on the screen with one soft diagonal sheen, in place of the flat cyan and ring. Make the bezel dark grey with a satin light edge on the top left; keep the home button.
- p12: Sneakers: Replace the house badge on the ear cup with a plain orange disc. Make the purple backdrop one even tone, with no lighter patch behind the helmet. Shade the white shell with cool grey, not purple; keep the gold visor and the orange trim.
