## Art director

Close. Rendering, finish and colour handling mostly match the real cards, and the mask and broom could pass as they are. Most of the giveaway comes from the horror theme being rendered literally. There are night or dusk skies with moons and stars, dark wood, near-black heroes and spotlight vignettes, while every real card is bright and daylit with an evenly lit backdrop. A smaller tell is weathered texture on supporting surfaces (nails, cracks, moss, hatched bark, inked bricks) and invented props (a gazebo, a mossy plinth). The fixes are colour, value and backdrop edits only. Several specialists' calls on the real cards were overruled: the scarf, bag, music player, topiary and vending machine. That shows weave, droplets, dense leaves and puffy forms are allowed in this style.

1. **Spooky low-key mood: night skies, dusk purple, dark wood and dark vignettes (pumpkin, arched door, purple book, witch hat, dead tree). All the real cards here are bright daytime or high-key studio scenes with light backdrops that stay even out to the corners. This is the strongest giveaway.** Set each scene in bright daylight or on an evenly bright backdrop. Use a clear blue sky, light honey-coloured wood and an evenly lit stripe or ring field out to the corners. The hero sits as a mid value inside it.
2. **Near-black or grey heroes and supporting surfaces (black spider, charcoal witch hat, grey-lavender stone wall). Real heroes keep saturated colour, even in shadow.** Paint dark objects in a deep colour such as plum, purple-blue or warm brown, with coloured shadows and one soft sheen from the upper left. Paint stone in warm sandy tones.
3. **Invented mood props the subject doesn't name: moon and stars, a gazebo, and a mossy, cracked stone plinth. Overrule on bases in general: the real topiary stands on a stone ring and the real fountain on a paved circle, so the problem is the props and the texture, not the fact that there is a base.** Keep only the hero and a plain ground or backdrop. Where a base stays, make it smooth, warm and simple.
4. **Weathered or fine detail on supporting surfaces: nail heads and worn grey streaks on planks, cracks and moss on stone, hatched bark lines, every brick inked. Overrule the surface and edge calls on the real cards: the canvas weave and stitching on the bag, the droplets on the vending machine, the dense leaves on the topiary and the ivy on the wreath door are all real, so detail on the hero itself is allowed.** Draw floors, tables, walls and bark as smooth, broad shapes with a few faint, soft grain or mortar lines.
5. **Studio spotlight look on single objects: a bright floor pool, darkened top corners and a long hard cast shadow (hourglass, witch hat). The real vending machine also sits on a studio floor but is lit evenly, so the problem is the spotlight, not the studio floor.** Use soft daylight from the upper left, a short soft contact shadow and an evenly bright backdrop.
6. **Overruled: the shape and proportion findings (swollen forms, thick legs, puffy lids). Form can't be judged across different objects, and the same 'puffy' look appears on the real scarf, ring box and music player. Composition calls for 'centred whole object' are weak too, because the real scarf, vending machine and mask are also centred and shown whole. Neither goes into edit notes.** No change.
