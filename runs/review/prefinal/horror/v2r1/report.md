## Art director

Close. The witch hat and the mask already pass, and the clock and the window are near misses. The main tell is grunge texture (weathered chipped wood, pitted iron, speckled stone) on the key, the cat's ledge, the candy jar and the pumpkin. After that come near-colorless dark heroes (key, cat, lamp post) and dense ground fields (grass blades, cobbles) that crowd a small hero (broom, lamp post). The two controls show that full garden scenes, marble veining, glow behind the hero and puffy shapes are all in the real style, so I overruled the staging, viewpoint and shape findings built on those.

1. **Grunge and realistic wear on surfaces: chipped, weathered planks with nail heads (candy jar, pumpkin), pitted, speckled iron (key) and gritty speckled stone (cat's ledge). No real card in the set has this kind of wear. Real wood is clean, warm and saturated (binoculars planks, rocking-chair floor), and real metal is smooth with broad highlights (brass lamp). This is the strongest tell that repeats across cards.** Draw wood as clean, warm, saturated planks with faint quiet grain. Draw metal and stone as smooth surfaces with broad soft gradients and one or two clean highlights.
2. **Dark heroes lose their color: the iron key, the black cat and the black lamp post read as near-colorless grey or black, and their shadows go dull. Real heroes stay saturated even when dark, like the witch-hat-style purple-black, the cobalt snorkel and the green binoculars.** Give dark objects a clear hue, such as warm bronze, deep green or blue-violet. Put a lighter colored top plane on them, lit from the upper left, and keep color in the shadows.
3. **Ground fields are drawn as dense repeated small units: grass blade by blade with clover sprigs (broom), and a whole plaza of identical cobbles (lamp post). The ground then competes with a small hero. Real grounds use larger, calmer units. The real fountain's tiles are big, and the tufts on the caravan's grass are sparse.** Draw the ground as a few large, soft shapes with broad shading, only a light hint of texture, and lower contrast than the hero.
4. **Overruled claims, falsified by the real cards. The controls show a real topiary in a full garden scene with a path, a hedge and hundreds of leaves, and a real marble lion with veins, trees, a path and its whole plinth. Real cards also include a bright glow behind the lamp, the sneakers' split wall and floor, the motel's near-black night sky, the beige fountain on yellow, the cream caravan, and the puffy ring box, binoculars and snorkel. So the following are not tells, and I am not acting on them: 'camera pulled back / whole object on a plinth / extra scenery', marble veining, glow patches on sunbursts, dark night skies, and all of the inflation and proportion findings. The staging_detail report also misaligned p11 and p12.** No style change.

- p01: Key: Paint the key as smooth warm aged bronze with copper-orange and brown tones, in place of the gritty pitted grey iron. Give it one or two clean highlight bands along the top of the ring and shaft, and a slightly darker lower-right side. Add a soft, faint shadow below and to the right; keep the green sunburst and the diagonal pose.
- p02: Grandfather Clock: Draw the clock slightly larger so it fills most of the card height. Make the green backdrop one even tone out to the corners, with no bright pool of light on the floor. Make the floor shadow short and soft; keep the wood case, the face and the gold pendulum.
- p03: keep
- p04: Rocking Chair: Draw the stone ledge as smooth, warm grey blocks with soft edges and no speckle or pitting. Give the cat soft blue-violet highlights on its head, shoulders and haunch from the upper left. Calm the pink glow behind the cat slightly; keep the eyes, the ears and the purple stripes.
- p05: keep
- p06: Binoculars: Make the grass a smooth, slightly deeper green lawn with soft broad shading and only a few blade hints, with no clovers. Add a soft, defined shadow on the grass beside the broom. Group the bristles into a few broad bundles; keep the red binding and the wooden handle.
- p07: Candy Bowl: Make the table clean, warm, honey-coloured wood planks with faint quiet grain, with no chipped paint, scratches or nail heads. Give the glass a slightly brighter rim highlight; keep the candies and the blue ring backdrop.
- p09: Motel: Replace the small cobbles with a few large, soft warm grey-tan paving stones that fade out away from the lamp. Paint the post a deep green metal with a brass trim. Let the lantern's warm light spill slightly down the post and onto the nearby stones; keep the lantern and the dusk sky.
- p10: Fountain: Make the table clean, warm wood planks with faint quiet grain, with no chipped paint, scratches or nail heads. Make the pumpkin skin smooth and glossy with no scratchy streaks; keep the glowing face, the stem and the blue stripes.
- p11: Cobweb Window: Let the candle cast a slightly warm glow onto the inner frame and the sill. Make the glass a deep blue with one soft reflection, in place of flat black. Keep the stone wall, the cobweb and the night sky.
