## Art director

Partly close. The rendering language is right: glossy toy 3D and the sunburst and concentric backdrops pass at a glance, and the helmet and planet could pass. The batch gives itself away on the sci-fi subjects. Grey or white metal and clear-glass heroes have no local colour and sit on photographic grey moon dust or concrete scattered with pebbles and craters (rover, moon base, console, tube, rocket floor). On top of that, glows tint whole cards (UFO, cockpit), and the meteor's tail is a smear. The redraw notes cover colour, ground and finish. The biggest remaining tell is that heroes are framed small and centred, where real cards are close and cropped, and notes can't fix that: it has to change in the generator's framing settings. Specialist flags on the two control pairs were wrong, so treat the edges and staging reports with some caution.

1. **Grey, colourless sci-fi heroes. The rover, console, moon base and stasis tube are painted in plain grey metal, white plastic or clear glass, and they sit on a grey ground of nearly the same value. Real cards give the hero one saturated colour of its own (blue vending machine, green binoculars, yellow submarine) against a backdrop in a contrasting colour. This one root cause covers the low subject chroma, the weak figure–ground separation and the flat, evenly lit grey faces the specialists reported.** Paint metal and glass sci-fi objects in a saturated main colour (orange, teal, red, blue) with silver or white trim. Use a ground or backdrop clearly darker or lighter than the hero, and in a contrasting hue.
2. **Photographic, speckled ground. The moon plains (p01, p08) are covered in evenly sized craters, pebbles and pits, and the rocket stands on gritty concrete (p02). Real cards use smooth painted surfaces with a few large, quiet shapes: wood planks, soft gradients, plain ledges. This also explains the reports of a sharp background with no edge hierarchy and too much environment detail.** Draw the ground as a smooth painted surface in one soft tone, with at most two or three large soft craters or seams and no scattered pebbles or grit.
3. **The hero is staged small, centred and fully in frame, with wide empty margins (rover, rocket, moon base, tube, UFO). Real cards push in close and often crop the hero. I can see this on the cards, and it is the strongest tell at thumbnail size. It is a camera and framing problem, so redraw notes cannot fix it. It needs to go to the generator's framing settings.** Frame the hero large, filling most of the card, with a slightly low three-quarter camera. Let it touch or crop at one card edge, with only a thin strip of floor or sky.
4. **A glow or tint takes over the card. The magenta halo washes over the UFO, purple tints every surface in the cockpit, and a cyan glow pools under the tube. The glow lights the hero from several directions, and the shadow side is lost. Earlier attempts to just calm a glow lost to their drafts, so the fix should set clean local colours and one key light rather than simply remove the glow.** Keep surfaces in their own local colours, lit by one soft key from the upper left with a darker side. Keep glowing lights as small accents.
5. **Smeared effects and over-busy metal finish. The meteor's tail is a wispy smear, and the UFO has brushed streaks across the whole disc with evenly spread crisp rivets. Real cards render effects as clean shapes and keep small hardware few and soft.** Draw trails and effects as smooth, clean-edged shapes. Give metal one broad soft highlight with only a few soft rivets.
6. **Overruled: the shape_proportions report as a whole (each pair shows a different object, so form cannot be compared). Also overruled: the edge claim of heavy dark outlines on the helmet, which I can't see on the card, and every flag on the controls. p10B (clock) and p12 (photo card and bird feeder) are real cards, which shows the edges, staging, lighting and finish specialists over-flag busy backgrounds and clean icons. I keep the planet and the helmet, because the reported tells there are weak or not visible.** No change.

- p01: Moon Rover: Paint the rover body bright orange with white trim, and the tyres dark grey instead of black. Smooth the ground into soft grey-beige dust with two or three large soft craters and no scattered pebbles or small pits.
- p02: Rocket: Replace the concrete floor with a smooth painted floor in soft cool blue, with no grit or seams. Give the white body a soft glossy sheen, lit from the upper left so its right side is slightly cooler and darker. Keep the orange-yellow stripes.
- p03: Control Console: Paint the console body a saturated teal-blue metal with silver trim, and keep the red buttons. Replace the white slab with a plain soft floor in a slightly darker blue, with a gentle reflection.
- p04: keep
- p05: Astronaut: Make the cabin walls and seat a light neutral grey-white, and keep purple only in the small ceiling and wall lights. Shade the skin with warm brown and the yellow trousers with darker yellow-orange, not violet.
- p06: keep
- p07: UFO: Paint the saucer as smooth soft grey metal with one broad highlight on top and no brushed streaks, and use fewer rivets. Light it from the upper left so the lower and far edge of the rim is slightly darker and cooler.
- p08: Moon Base: Paint the dome white with a bold teal stripe and the connecting tube bright yellow. Smooth the ground into soft blue-grey dust, slightly darker than the dome, with one or two large soft craters and no scattered pebbles or pits.
- p09: Meteor: Give the rock a grey stone colour with a darker lower side and craters lit from one direction. Draw the tail as a smooth pale blue trail with clean edges and no wispy streaks. Replace the wavy ribbons with soft orange sunburst rays.
- p11: Stasis Tube: Fill the glass with softly glowing orange liquid. Paint the cap and base ring a saturated blue with silver trim. Remove the cyan glow on the floor and add a soft dark contact shadow under the base.
