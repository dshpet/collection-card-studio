## Art director

Close. Eight of the ten generated cards could pass next to the real ones as they are. The main tell left is value: the Evidence Bag's dark mahogany and the Steel Safe's near-black navy make those cards darker than the real set's light, mid-key look. I gave each a small fix, reusing the previously kept Evidence Bag note. The Squad Car's extra street scenery is a weak tell that a note can't fix. Many specialist flags landed on real cards (stage, sneakers, vending machine, truss) or asked for proportion changes, so I overruled them. Style text unchanged.

1. **Dark, low-key surfaces around or on the hero. The Evidence Bag sits on near-black mahogany, and the Steel Safe is a near-black navy block. Both cards read darker than the real set, which keeps light, mid-key backdrops and mid-value heroes. This is the clearest tell, and the color and value numbers back it on both cards.** Lighten the wood under the bag to warm honey wood. Raise the safe slightly to a mid blue with brighter top edges. Leave the backdrop hues as they are.
2. **Extra scenery around a fully framed hero. The Squad Car has a house, bushes and clouds behind it. This is a weak tell: real cards also use scene backdrops (the bike's sunset road, the stage, the doorway). Only the camera crop could fix it, and edit notes can't change that.** No edit. The card can pass as it is. Removing the house would risk a worse redraw for little gain.
3. **Overruled calls. Many specialist flags point at real cards: the guitar stage, the sneakers, the vending machine, the stage-light truss, the rocking chair's grain, the tennis racket strings and the sunset bike scene. Those prove the real set does carry busy scenes, glossy lacquer and dense detail, so none of them become rules. All shape, thickness and proportion fixes are also out of scope. Edits already tried and judged worse are not repeated: the flashlight's white streaks and the wallet's stitching.** Don't push toward smoother, plainer or less detailed surfaces. The real cards have that detail too.
