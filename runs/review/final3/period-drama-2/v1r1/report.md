## Art director

Close. Six of the ten generated cards could pass as real: parasol, hatbox, teacup, pocket watch, top hat and fan. The remaining tells are mostly in the setting rather than the hero: busy invented floors and cloths (trunk, cake stand), theatrical or all-warm light and value (inkwell, piano), and over-glossy finishes. The four edit notes each make one to three small changes to backdrop, light or color, and leave every hero's form alone. The Piano and Cake Stand notes are narrower than the earlier attempts that lost, since those changed too much at once.

1. **The model invents busy settings with fine texture around the hero: cracked flagstones and a yellow platform strip under the trunk, a finely wrinkled tablecloth under the cake stand, and mirror reflections on the piano floor. In the real cards the backdrop is a few large, quiet shapes, and the floor matches the backdrop color.** Under the hero, draw a broad, smooth floor or cloth with a few soft joints or folds, in a color close to the backdrop. Keep the sharpest detail on the hero.
2. **Some cards push value and light to theatrical extremes. The inkwell has a hard key light, a near-black side and a long, sharp cast shadow. The piano is a deep, glossy red-brown against a pale cream wall. Real cards use one soft key light, a shadow side in mid-dark tones, and short, soft contact shadows.** Use soft light from the upper left. Keep the shadow side a rich, deep version of the object's own color, and make cast shadows short and soft.
3. **Some cards drift warm. Whites turn cream (the feather), and walls, floors and the hero all fall into one orange or red family (piano, inkwell). Real cards set a warm hero against a cooler or contrasting backdrop.** Keep whites clean, with soft grey-blue shading. When the hero is warm, give the wall a cooler or contrasting hue.
4. **Wood and metal are over-finished: lacquered swirly grain on the piano and mirror-gold on the watch, with a hard highlight on every link. Real cards use satin surfaces with one broad highlight per part.** Paint wood and metal with a satin finish and one broad, soft highlight per part. Keep the grain faint.
5. **Several generated heroes are framed whole, centered and front-on, with wide even margins (hatbox, teacup, cake stand, suitcase-style framing). This is a mild tell only: the real controls also include centered objects on plain gradients. Specialists' requests to reshape parts (fan ribs, hat brim, parasol frill, chain length) are overruled, because form can't be judged across different objects.** No edit notes for this. Leave framing to the style references.
