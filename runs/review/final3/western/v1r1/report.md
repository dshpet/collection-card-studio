## Art director

Close. The specialists did little better than chance: per pair, each picked the generated card in only 2 to 6 of the 10 pairs. Many of their 'tells' are traits the real cards share: centred spotlight staging (trophy), candy hues and dark outlines (fan, gloves), the hero repeating the backdrop's hue (mortarboard), cream whites (caravan), and sand with pebbles under a toy (duck). Seven of the ten generated cards could pass as they are: star, harmonica, hat, boots, cactus, bandana and saddle. The windmill is the one card that clearly gives itself away, with all nine specialists picking it. Its grass-strand field and scattered rocks are fine texture that the real cards never use. The campfire and the wagon share the same root cause, smaller in scale: invented clutter and props around the hero (pebbles, a cabin, a kettle). All three notes are limited to ground, backdrop and props, with at most three changes each.

1. **Busy ground under the hero: the windmill field is drawn as dense individual grass strands with scattered rocks, the wagon's dirt is full of evenly spread pebbles and specks, and the campfire ground has pebbles and grass clumps. On the real cards the ground is a few large, smooth, softly shaded shapes, so this fine scattered texture is the clearest tell.** Paint the ground as one smooth, softly shaded surface in broad tones, with a single soft contact shadow under the hero. Where small stones or tufts appear, use only one or two large, soft ones.
2. **Extra props that compete with the hero: the campfire comes with a log cabin, a kettle on its own stone and pine trees, so the first read is split. Real cards can show a setting, like the binoculars' balcony and city, but the setting stays soft and only the hero is a solid object.** Keep one hero object. Turn any second object into the soft setting behind it: trees, sky or a wall in broad simple shapes.
3. **Small added surface detail on leather and wood: dashed stitch lines around the saddle's edges, bolt caps on its strap, decorative stitching on the boots, and rivets and bolted hubs on the wagon. This is a weak tell. Real cards also carry readable detail, such as the jacket's badges and the phone booth's keypad, so only ask for slightly less.** Where a note asks for it, make the leather and wood surfaces slightly smoother, with fewer stitch lines and plain bands in place of bolts.
4. **Overruled: centred, front-on, symmetric staging on a plinth or under a spotlight. The real trophy is exactly that, and the real buoy is centred and front-on too. Do not use this to flag the star or the hat.** No change. Front-on centred heroes are part of this game's look.
5. **Overruled: saturated candy hues, the hero repeating the backdrop's hue, cream whites, and dark even outlines. On the real cards the fan is pure violet with a dark contour, the gloves are full red, the mortarboard is navy on blue rings, the chair is orange on an orange floor, the caravan is cream, and the sneakers have rainbow trim.** No change. Keep the current saturation and outline weight.
6. **Overruled: mixing a real ground with an abstract graphic backdrop, using a backdrop motif unrelated to the subject, putting a toy on sand with pebbles, and showing blotchy rings or a glowing halo. The real caravan, phone booth, duck, pillow and fan show all of these.** No change. Ground-plus-pattern backdrops and soft glows behind the hero stay allowed.
7. **Overruled: every comment on proportion, thickness, inflation and solidity. The shape specialist picked the generated card in only 4 of 10 pairs, and form can't be compared across different objects.** No change to forms.
