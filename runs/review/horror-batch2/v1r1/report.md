## Art director

Close to the real cards, but not quite there. The ghost and the bat could pass and are kept, and the tree and the door are only a step away. The cards that give themselves away share four habits. First, dark heroes go near-black and some scenes drop to a low key (the hat and the cauldron are the worst). Second, the backgrounds use photoreal weathered planks or bricks (pumpkin, potion, porch, dungeon). Third, there is a glow halo behind the hero or darkened corners. Fourth, a few heroes get flat frontal light or invented extras (the castle, porch clutter, a star tag). Most specialists judged the real rowboat control as generated. Their objections to chunky forms, orange wood, lily pads and dense leaves were overruled, because the real cards have all of these. The centered, uncropped framing is a real but secondary tell that the notes don't try to fix.

1. **Dark heroes and dark scenes. The witch hat is a near-black shape on pale cyan. The cauldron is a black pot in a dim purple dungeon. The bat and the tree trunk lean dark too. The real cards keep a bright key and a mid-value hero, and even dark materials get colored light faces and a lit rim (see the binoculars and the vending machine).** Paint dark objects in a deep color (plum, blue-grey iron) with lighter colored lit faces and a bright rim. Keep the scene bright, and use black only in thin crease shadows.
2. **Photoreal weathered surfaces behind the hero: peeling, grimy planks with nail heads and moss under the pumpkin and the potion, a peeling porch door and scuffed boards on the Selfie card, and cracked, outlined bricks and flagstones around the cauldron. This is the strongest texture tell. The real wood (barrel deck, binocular floor, dock) is smooth and warm with a few soft grain lines.** Draw tables, floors, doors and walls as smooth, clean, warm-toned planks or blocks with a few soft seams. Leave out peeling paint, nails, moss and cracks.
3. **A bright glow or halo right behind the hero, plus darkened corners: pumpkin, potion and crystal ball, with a strong vignette on the Selfie porch. Real backdrops are lit evenly, and their rays and rings stay tone-on-tone.** Light the backdrop evenly from edge to edge, with no glow circle behind the object and no darkened corners.
4. **Flat, frontal light with weak cast shadows on the door and the tree, and a hard white window highlight on the crystal ball. Real cards have one clear key from the upper left, a darker shadow side and soft cast shadows (the topiary and the wreath door show this well).** Light from the upper left. Give the right side a clearly darker shadow and add a soft cast shadow on the ground or wall.
5. **Invented extra props the subject never asked for: the castle tower behind the tree, ivy and a pumpkin on the porch, and a star tag on the potion.** Show only the named subject and its natural setting. Replace extras with soft, simple background masses.
6. **The hero is centered, front-on and fully inside the frame with generous even margins (door with its doorstep, crystal ball, pumpkin, potion). Real cards often crop or turn the object. This is real but secondary, and edit notes can't safely fix it, so it is not in any note.** No edit notes for this. Note it as a pattern for future generation only.
7. **Overruled: every shape_proportions call, plus the calls that pinned in-style features on generated cards. Both controls prove the real cards are chunky, toy-like and densely detailed. Most specialists marked the real rowboat (p03 B) as generated for its lily pads, ripples, orange wood and plank grain, and called the real binoculars inflated. The topiary's glossy stamped leaves, the dense ivy on the blue door and the jacket's lighting are all from real cards. None of these are tells.** Change nothing about form, roundness, chunkiness, saturated orange wood or dense foliage. These are part of the real style.

- p01: Haunted Door: Light from the upper left so the arch stones cast soft shadows down onto the wooden planks and the right side of each stone is darker. Make the stone wall a slightly richer lavender-blue instead of grey. Keep the door as is.
- p02: Witch Hat: Make the hat a deep plum-purple felt instead of black, with soft violet lit faces on the crown and brim and rich indigo shadows. Make the background rays a slightly deeper teal. Keep the hat shape and band.
- p04: Cargo Barrel (pumpkin): Make the table smooth warm-brown planks with a few soft grain lines and no peeling paint, scratches or nail heads. Make the blue sky even, with no glow behind the pumpkin. Keep the pumpkin as is.
- p05: Selfie: Remove the pumpkin and the ivy leaves. Paint the door and porch boards in smooth, clean colors with no peeling paint or grain scratches. Light the whole porch evenly so the corners are not darkened.
