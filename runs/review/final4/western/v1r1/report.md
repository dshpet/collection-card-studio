## Art director

Close. All three generated cards match the real cards on rendering, palette and finish. What gives them away is staging: invented props and scattered clutter (the campfire kettle and cabin, the pebbles and flowers on the wagon road), dense crisp ground texture (the wheat field is the worst offender), and small hardware details (rivets and bolts). The windmill is furthest off, because the wheat dominates and the hero is small. The campfire and the wagon should pass once the clutter is removed. The specialists' color and cropping complaints are overruled, because the real controls (chess table, soccer ball, beach ball) show those traits are on-style.

1. **Invented extra props and scattered ground clutter around the hero: a blue kettle on the campfire stones, a log cabin with a lit window, pebbles, a grey rock and white flowers on the wagon road. Real cards have one hero and plain ground. The real chess table sits in a full park scene, but that scene has no stray objects.** Show only the named object on plain, smooth ground. The ground is broad soft color areas: dirt, grass or sand with a few large tufts or ripples. The background holds only the setting the object naturally lives in: trees, hills, sky.
2. **Dense, repeated, crisp ground texture: hundreds of separately drawn wheat ears, plus speckles of grass blades and pebbles. This makes the ground as sharp and busy as the hero and turns the value into light speckle. Real grounds (soccer grass, beach sand, dock water) are broad, soft masses with a few accents.** Paint fields and ground as smooth broad color bands with soft shading, picking out only a few large stalks, tufts or ripples near the hero.
3. **Small hard hardware added to the hero: rivets at every crossing of the windmill lattice and bolt heads on the wagon box corners. Real cards simplify surfaces to smooth satin with broad highlights.** Draw wood and painted metal as smooth satin surfaces with broad, soft highlights and plain joints.
4. **A slightly harsh side light: the windmill lattice's right faces go dark brown and the wagon throws a long, crisp cast shadow. This is a minor tell. Real cards also show cast shadows (the soccer ball and the chess table), so only soften it slightly.** Keep the shadow sides of the hero a warm mid-tone and the cast shadows soft at the edges.
5. **Overruled: the palette complaints (pure hues, cyan water, orange sand) and most viewpoint and cropping demands. The color, value and several composition specialists picked real cards: the coaster, the viewer, the dock, the beach ball and the chess table. The real controls show that saturated grass and sand, fully framed centered heroes, and tree-and-path backgrounds are all on-style. Proportion and roundness notes are excluded by the rules. The windmill's small size in the frame is a real outlier, but it is a matter of view, so it is left to generation.** No change: keep the current saturation, the framing habits and the full-scene backgrounds.

- p01: Covered Wagon: Replace the pebbles, the grey rock and the white flowers with a smooth, plain dirt road and soft grass with a few broad tufts. Make the shadow under the wagon slightly shorter and softer. Paint the wagon box as smooth planks with no bolt heads. Keep the wagon, the red wheels, the canvas cover, the hills, the sky and the view.
- p02: Campfire: Remove the blue kettle and show a plain stone in its place. Replace the log cabin with soft pine trees and bushes. Replace the scattered pebbles with smooth, plain warm dirt. Keep the fire, the logs, the stone ring, the grass tufts and the view.
- p03: Windmill: Replace the field of individual wheat ears with a smooth golden wheat mass in a few broad, soft bands, with only a few stalks drawn along the bottom edge. Paint the tower beams smooth matte red-brown with no rivets, and make their right faces slightly lighter. Keep the windmill, the white wheel, the yellow vane, the sky and the view.
