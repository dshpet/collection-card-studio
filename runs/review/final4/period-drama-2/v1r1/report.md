## Art director

Yes, it's close. Most specialists who found a fault picked the real cards, especially on light, color, finish and staging. The candelabra's palette, lighting and finish match the real look. What is left is mild: the hero is small in a large, plain teal field, and there is a little extra ring detail on the stem and base. Neither is worth the risk of an edit, so keep the card.

1. **Small hero in a big, flat, empty field. The candelabra is upright and narrow, so wide bands of plain teal sit on either side, and the dark top band leaves the hero looking a little lost at thumbnail size. Real cards fill the frame and put a soft glow behind the hero. This is the one tell that is clearly visible on p01 A, and it is mild.** For tall, thin objects, stage the hero large and close, and put a soft light glow on the backdrop right behind it so the field isn't one empty flat gradient.
2. **Many even ring grooves on the stem and concentric rings on the base, plus a crisp rim around the flames. This is the generator's usual habit of over-crisp, repeated small edges, but it is subtle on this card, and the rule is not to edit form.** No edit. Watch for it in the style: keep hard edges for the silhouette and let small repeated details stay soft.
3. **Overruled. Several specialists picked the real control cards (sneakers, flip-flops, keyboard, bumper car, bottle) for flat front light, neon accents, a horizon line, a mirror floor or busy seaweed. All six of those cards are real, so none of these points describes the generated look. Viewpoint and shape calls on the candelabra were weak and about form, so they are overruled too.** Don't change anything based on these points. Real cards can have wall/floor horizons, scenery props, patterned icon backdrops, saturated multi-color accents and even front light.
