## Art director

Close, but not yet passing. The rendering family is right, and no card is a hard miss, yet every generated card is still identifiable, so all eight get a redraw. Whistle and Bugle need the least. The main problem is that the last correction went too far: heroes are now smaller, slimmer, crisper and tidier than the real cards. The new text brings back scale and plump soft forms, and drops 'distant camera' and 'stay slim'.

Checked against the answer key, I trusted viewpoint_depth, lighting_shading and composition_hierarchy (7 of 8 each) and surface_finish (6 of 8, one abstained, wrong on p08).

Overruled: shape_proportions (2 of 8) preferred the slimmer generated forms on p01, p03, p04, p07 and p08, so its 'thin it down' fixes are rejected. value_contrast, edges_linework and staging_detail (3 of 8 each) and color_palette (4 of 8) repeatedly marked real cards as odd. Rejected from them: removing the whistle's glow, the cup's vignette and big steam, the barrel's plank walls and cyan sky, and the dark forests; thinning the rims; tightening the tent; removing the pot's soot; and muting the whistle and basket colour. composition_hierarchy's p02 'chunkier bugle' and viewpoint_depth's p01 call are also rejected. The glow, soot, cyan sky and saturation they objected to are real traits, so do not correct them away.

1. **The hero is too small and sits complete, centred and contained, like a tidy diorama. This is the strongest tell. The tea cup, basket, barrel and whistle are small with wide empty margins, and the campfire cook and tent show the whole scene neatly framed. The real cards push the hero large and close, touching or cropped by the edge. The old 'distant camera' wording drives this, so it is removed.** Draw the subject big and close, filling the card and nearly touching its edges. Let the card edge crop the ground, logs or a corner of the subject. Bugle is the exception: see its note.
2. **The slimness rule has over-corrected. Handles, hoops, rims, rings and weave are now thinner and fussier than on the real cards. The real tea cup has a thick lip and handle, the real basket has a fat handle and wide weave, the real barrel has heavy hoops, and the real whistle is plump. The generated versions look dry and precise like product renders, not soft game art.** Replace the slim-parts sentence. Build each object from a few large, plump, soft parts with true proportions. Keep real thin parts, such as the bugle tube and cords, thin only where the subject needs them.
3. **Surplus crisp micro-detail: dense weave, tyre tread, seat pleats, steering wheel, ribbed grille, speckled asphalt, dashed stitches, rope lashings, shiny leaves, flowers, stone ring, bubbles on tea. The real cards keep these areas simple and soft, and these additions read as a different hand.** Materials look true but smooth, with one broad soft sheen. Large plain surfaces carry the card. Add only the parts the subject names.
4. **Light is cool, shallow and uncommitted on the hero. The generated cup and barrel stay near one flat value, and the pot is an even balloon. The real cards turn hard from a bright side into a deep saturated shadow, with surrounding colour bouncing in: red-orange in the whistle shadow, pink in the cup, warm reflection in the steel hoops, firelight on the pot belly.** One light side turns smoothly into a deep, saturated shadow, and surrounding color glows into the object. Whites and metals pick up the color around them.
5. **Backgrounds are flat and do not sit with the hero. Generated walls are one even tone with hard shadows and little light on them. Real settings have soft glow behind the hero, gradients and warm reflections on the ground, and the forest scenes are dark and deep rather than yellow-lit. Hue drift is card-specific: the bugle burst is too neon, the taxi burst too pale, and the barrel sky too dull, so this stays out of the style rule.** Keep the setting rich in color and softly lit behind the subject, with gentle gradients and glow. Keep the card's category of background.
6. **Ground and space errors on single cards: the barrel deck bends like a dome, the tent floor tips up toward the camera, and the taxi and bugle are pushed into toy-like proportions by a stronger lens.** Handle per card in the redraw notes. No style rule needed.

Style text:

The attached images are real cards from the game. Draw one new card that could sit beside them unnoticed: the same rendering, lighting, finish, color and detail.
The subject is big and close, filling the card, nearly touching its edges; let it crop where natural. Forms are plump and soft with true proportions, built from few large parts. One light side turns smoothly into a deep, saturated shadow. Surrounding color glows into the object. Materials look true but smooth, with one broad soft sheen. The setting stays rich in color, softly lit behind the subject.
Draw only what the subject names. No text, letters, numbers, frame, border or watermark.

- p01: Whistle: closest of the set, small fix. Draw it about 20% larger and plumper in a hot, smooth orange, with shadows and openings going to saturated red. Smooth satin surface. Paler yellow field with a soft bright glow around the whistle, in place of the cast shadow.
- p02: Bugle: this one stays slender. Smaller bell, thin brass tube turned at a three-quarter angle, double coil with a gap, thin cord with small narrow tassels. Light from the upper left, soft dark on the lower coil. Soft muted teal rays, even across the card.
- p03: Tea Cup: draw it much larger, wide across the card, with a thick soft lip and a plump handle. Bowl turns strongly from bright to shaded, with pink-red bounce in the white and the saucer. Plain tea with one soft sheen. Seamless red backdrop with a soft glow behind the cup, darker corners, and a tall soft steam plume.
- p04: Basket: draw it 1.5 times larger, filling the card, with big apples, a thick twisted handle and wide plump weave strips. Saturated orange-red wicker. Simple apples with no leaves. Soft lit wall falling off to one side, warm ledge with a short soft shadow and a warm reflection.
- p05: Cargo Barrel: draw it large and close, filling the card height, with heavy steel hoops carrying warm reflections. Strong round shading, deep gaps between staves, and fine grain that differs stave to stave. Lower camera. Straight deck planks receding to one point, plank walls at both sides, bright turquoise sky.
- p06: Taxi: longer, lower sedan with a real wheelbase and smaller wheels, close to the card edge, bumper a single soft bar. Soft yellow paint with one broad sheen, soft plain windscreen, roof sign sitting on the cabin. Smooth dark violet-grey road with lane dashes running diagonally off the card. Deep blue burst.
- p07: Tent: draw it large and off-centre with one corner touching the card edge. Soft golden canvas with slack, gently sagging lines, one big roof plane in cool shade, thick rolled door flaps, dark opening with no floor showing. Deep blue-green forest with dark red-brown trunks and pale mist behind. Plain grass, plain hems.
- p08: Campfire Cook: move in close. Wide pot as the main shape, with a large bright stew surface and a handle arching up to the beam. Fire glows orange on the belly and fades upward, with scorched soot marks at the base. Small fire of logs cropped by the bottom edge. Plain straight posts, dark deep forest behind.
