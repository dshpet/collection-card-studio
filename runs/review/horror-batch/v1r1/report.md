## Art director

This batch is close. Silhouettes, rendering softness and backdrop types (rings, sunbursts, ledges, grass patches) all match the real cards, and most generated cards would pass a quick glance. The giveaways are colour and story choices, not technique. Several horror props are drawn near-black or grey on louder neon backgrounds (hat, cauldron, cottage). A few cards stay in one hue family (bat on orange, all-orange pumpkin). Invented extras give some away (a pumpkin and motion lines beside the ghost sheep, a moon in a daytime sky, a falling leaf, daisies). The candle, pumpkin, candy corn and tree are lit flat from the front. The potion can be kept. Every other note is limited to colour, light, finish and removing extras, with no form or pose changes. Many specialist claims were overruled: all proportion and viewpoint fixes, the glow-behind-the-hero tell (real cards have it too), and every flag raised on the real control cards.

1. **Several heroes have almost no colour: near-black or grey bodies on loud, fully saturated backgrounds. The witch hat is black on lime, the cauldron is black on hot pink, and the cottage walls are grey-lavender on hard cyan. On the real cards the hero always keeps a rich colour of its own, like the navy cap, red mower, blue bottle and tan jacket, so the background never outshouts it. This is the biggest giveaway because it shows at thumbnail size.** Paint dark objects in a deep saturated colour, such as plum-purple felt or blue-violet iron, with a lit rim and coloured shadows, so the hero is the richest colour on the card.
2. **Invented story props and extras the real cards don't have: a second jack-o'-lantern and motion squiggles beside the ghost sheep, a moon in a daytime sky, a lone falling leaf, and small daisies scattered on the grass. Real cards show the object alone in a quiet set.** Show the hero alone. Draw the grass as a smooth, even green with a few large tufts and the sky with soft clouds only.
3. **Some cards stay inside one hue family and their shadows only darken that same hue. The brown bat sits on an orange burst, and the pumpkin is orange throughout with orange shadows. Real cards set the hero against a contrasting or related background hue, and their shadows shift toward red-brown or violet.** Set warm heroes on a cool background and shade them with red-brown or violet shadows.
4. **Several cards are lit flat from the front with no clear dark side: the candle, pumpkin, candy corn and tree. Glowing parts like the flame and the carved face don't light the surfaces next to them. Real cards have one key light from the upper side, with one side clearly in shade and a contact shadow under the object.** Light from the upper left: one side bright, the other side clearly darker, with a soft contact shadow. Let glowing parts warm the surfaces next to them.
5. **Some surfaces carry photographic or added texture: a speckled grey concrete counter, scratches and mottling on the cauldron iron, and individual fur strands on the bat. Real materials are smooth with broad soft highlights.** Keep materials smooth and clean with broad soft highlights. Draw fur as a few soft clumps.
6. **Overruled. (a) All proportion, viewpoint and pose fixes, such as turning to 3/4, a thinner brim, slimmer legs or a wedge-shaped candy corn: form can't be judged across different objects, and pose changes are out of scope. (b) The bright glow behind the hero as a tell: the real cap, jacket and bottle cards all have a bright centre. (c) Flags raised on real cards: the p06 bottle lighting, p08 scroll values and coaster saturation, p05 topiary gloss, p12 gate gloss and p11 fruit droplets. Those cards are the reference. (d) The potion lime cork and purple spill: the real cards are this saturated too.** No change.

- p01: Witch Hat: Paint the hat in deep plum-purple felt with violet shadows instead of black, with a soft lighter sheen down the left of the cone. Make the green rings slightly softer and lighter, with no bright yellow-green core behind the brim.
- p02: Candlestick: Replace the grey speckled stone counter with a smooth warm honey-wood ledge. Let the flame cast a small warm glow on the candle top and the inside of the dish. Put the right side of the brass stem in clear shadow.
- p03: keep
- p04: Cauldron: Paint the pot dark blue-violet iron with a lit rim and a broad soft highlight on the upper left of the belly instead of solid black, with no scratches. Remove the daisies. Make the pink rings an even, slightly dustier rose with no bright spot behind the pot.
- p05: Spooky Tree: Remove the falling orange leaf. Light from the upper left so the right side of the trunk is slightly darker, and add a soft tree shadow on the grass to the right.
- p07: Jack-o'-lantern: Shade the rib grooves and the right side of the pumpkin with deep red-brown, keeping a broad sheen on the left ribs. Make the purple sunburst slightly lighter and less saturated.
- p09: Ghost Sheep: Remove the jack-o'-lantern and the motion lines; the sheep stands alone on the lawn. Make the sky a calm daytime blue with only a hint of lavender. Shade the sheet folds in soft blue-grey, slightly lighter.
- p10: Bat: Change the sunburst to cool violet-purple rays. Paint the wing membranes dusky violet-brown and draw the body fur as a few soft rounded clumps instead of strands.
- p11: Candy Corn: Light from the upper right so the left side of the candy is clearly darker, matching its cast shadow. Make the tip a warm waxy cream. Darken the wall stripes slightly so the tip is the brightest spot.
- p12: Haunted House: Remove the moon and the daisies. Paint the stone walls a warm lilac and the roofs deep plum, with the side wall and the right of the tower slightly darker. Make the sky and grass slightly softer and less saturated.
