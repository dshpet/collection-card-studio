## Art director

Close, but not there yet. Each card looks competent, and two (p02, p07) would pass. The other six read as a cleaner, more realistic neighbour of the game, not as its own cards. They are smaller, tidier, flatter-lit and less toy-like than the real ones. The main cause is our own previous style text, which over-corrected, so this round reverses it. Most specialists were misled by the same thing. Against the answer key, composition_hierarchy and lighting_shading were right on 6 of 8 pairs. Value_contrast and staging_detail got 1 of 8, and shape_proportions 1 of 8, with viewpoint_depth at 2 of 8. Their calls mostly picked the real card as the fake, so I overruled their fixes wherever they asked for slimmer parts, a longer lens, or fewer glows and darks. Risk: an earlier round likely ballooned objects, so 'slightly chunky' must be checked for inflation on the next run.

1. **The last correction went too far. Generated objects now look like sensible, realistic objects, while the real cards draw simplified, chunky toy versions. See the real taxi's short hood and bulging roof, the basket's fat rim, rope handle and huge apples, the thick porcelain cup, and the tent's heavy rolled flaps. Our own 'true real-world proportions, long lens, stay thin' rule caused this. I overrule shape_proportions and viewpoint_depth: on p01, p03, p04, p06, p07 and p08 they called the real card wrong, and their fixes would push further from the game. Exception: p02's generated bugle is fatter than the real one, so the rule should be a slight chunkiness, not inflation.** Draw the object simplified and slightly chunky, like a polished toy model, with rounded forms. Fewer, bigger parts. Slim cords and rings stay allowed.
2. **Heroes sit too small and too tidy. The generated cup, basket, barrel, taxi and pot-and-frame are contained, centred and surrounded by empty margin. The real cards push one big object toward the viewer, nearly to the edges, and sometimes crop it, as the real campfire crops its logs. Composition_hierarchy is the most reliable report here (6 of 8 calls right, with measured margins and areas). I overrule its p02 call: the smaller bugle is the real one, and the generated one needs no enlarging.** Frame the object close and large, nearly touching the card edges. Let one low part run off the edge when it suits the subject.
3. **Generated cards are lit flat and front-on. The barrel's staves barely turn, the cup is flat grey, the basket has a pasted firm oval shadow, and the whistle's shadows go dull brown. Real cards have one clear light, a deep shadow side, a smooth turn between them, and saturated shadow colour, such as red in the real whistle's slot. Lighting_shading is the other reliable report (6 of 8 right). I overrule it on p07 and p08, where the rolled flaps and glowing pot belly are real.** Light from one side: a bright side turning smoothly into a deep, saturated shadow side, with a soft shadow fading across the ground.
4. **Materials shine in the wrong places, and our rule caused it. 'Only metal and glaze shine' plus 'a few quiet marks' gave a dull scratched whistle and hard, tidy finishes. Real plastic, fruit and porcelain have a wet, soft gloss with one broad highlight.** Plastic, metal, glaze and fruit shine with one broad soft highlight. Remove the scratches and scuffs.
5. **Small realistic detail is over-resolved. Examples are the taxi's chrome, hood ornament, ribbed tyres, interior and asphalt grain, the barrel's rivets, the cup's tea bubbles and thin decal flower, and every crisp basket strand. Real cards use a few large shapes. I do not accept the blanket ban on texture: the real pot's soot stipple and the real barrel's long grain strokes show the game allows a bold large mark.** Surfaces stay smooth, with a few large details. Draw big simple shapes in place of fine trim and grain.
6. **Backdrops are too polite. Generated cards use level planks, hard table lines and tidy clearings. Real cards have atmosphere: a pale glow behind the whistle, a glowing wall and warm table reflection behind the basket, dark corners on the cup, near-black forest behind the tent, a tilted road, a raking deck. I overrule value_contrast, staging_detail and color_palette where they ask to remove glows, vignettes, darks or cyan skies, and where they demand cool neutrals: those are all features of the real cards. They would make the cards less like the game. Handle this per card, not as a style rule.** Give the backdrop a soft pool of light or deep darks behind the object where a redraw needs it. Avoid hard horizon lines.

Style text:

The attached images are real cards from the game. Draw one new card that could sit beside them unnoticed: the same rendering, lighting, finish, color and level of detail.
Draw the object simplified and slightly chunky, like a polished toy model, with rounded forms.
Frame it close and large, nearly touching the card edges.
Light it from one side: a bright side turning smoothly into a deep, saturated shadow side, with a soft fading ground shadow.
Plastic, metal, glaze and fruit shine with one broad soft highlight.
Surfaces stay smooth, with a few large details.
Draw only what the subject names. No text, letters, numbers, frame, border or watermark.

- p01: Glossy, wet-looking orange plastic with soft rounded edges and no scratches. Shadows in the slots and under the barrel turn rich saturated red. Add a soft pale glow behind the whistle.
- p02: keep
- p03: Draw the cup and saucer much larger, low in the card, with the saucer spanning most of the width. Thick soft porcelain with a rounded rim, a plump handle and a low body with no tall foot. Add a tall bold curl of steam rising almost to the top. Use one seamless deep red backdrop with darker corners and no table line.
- p04: Draw the basket much larger, filling the card. Three or four huge apples, a thick rope handle and a fat rolled rim wider than the body. Replace the firm oval shadow with a soft fading shadow and a warm glow on the table under the basket. Add a pale glowing patch on the purple wall.
- p05: Draw the barrel much larger and wider, seen closer and slightly from above, pressing toward the viewer. Strong side light: a bright band of staves, the other side in deep shadow. Plain bands with no rivets. Bright cyan sky, with deck planks running diagonally.
- p06: Redraw as a pudgy toy car: short hood, bulging roof, fat fenders, one thick plain bumper, a few broad grille bars, simple hubs and a lump for a mirror. The car nearly touches both edges. Smooth flat road with big bold dashes, on a slight tilt. Deep blue rays.
- p07: keep
- p08: Make the pot much larger, with its rim nearly as wide as the frame, as the first read. Remove the rock ring. Put a small fire under the pot with its logs cropped by the bottom edge. Red firelight glows up the pot's belly. Simple forked posts with no rope lashing. Bright dense pines behind.
