## Art director

Close to the real cards. The magnifier's shape, finish, edges and quiet ring backdrop already match the real hoop card. The main giveaway is the green-tinted glass, which repeats the backdrop color. The second is the flat frontal light with a halo shadow. A small edit for color and light should let it pass. Several specialists blamed the real control cards (the bike, the tote bag, and the hoop's outlines). Those calls are false positives, and I have set them aside.

1. **The magnifier's lens glass is tinted the same bright green as the ring backdrop. The hero repeats the background hue, so the card reads as one green mass and the lens looks empty. On the cards I can see that the real hoop keeps a neutral white board against its cyan field.** Tint the lens glass a pale, clear, cool blue-grey that is clearly different from the green rings. Keep one soft curved reflection along its upper left.
2. **The light is frontal and even. The silver ring is the same soft value all the way round, and the shadow is a soft halo all around rather than one cast in a single direction. The real hoop has a lit upper-left side, a darker cool lower-right side, and a directional cast shadow.** Light from the upper left. Make the lower-right part of the metal ring and the underside of the red handle slightly darker. Cast a soft, faint shadow of the magnifier down and to the right onto the rings.
3. **The magnifier is fairly small and centred, with even green space on every side. This is a mild sign. The real hoop fills more of the card and is cropped by the card edge. This is minor, so leave it out of the note so the edit stays within three changes. Overruled: the request to tilt the lens into an oval and add a ground plane (viewpoint_depth), because it contradicts the producer's note that the lens faces the viewer as a near circle. Also overruled: the calls on the real control cards (bike, tote bag, hoop outline), because those cards are real.** No change in this round. Keep the view, the near-circular lens facing the viewer, and the handle running down to the right.
