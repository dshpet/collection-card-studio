## Art director

Close. Most generated cards could pass at a glance: the dish, planet and rocket are kept as they are, and the rest need small fixes. What gives the rest away is staging and colour, not rendering: invented clay-mound or crater terrain (rover, telescope, moon base), steel floor (console), single-family or neutral palettes (console, moon base, helmet), and an even lacquer gloss with scattered small rivets and pits. Overruled: all shape and proportion fixes and all camera changes are out of scope per the brief. The lighting reads on p09 and p11, colour on p05 and p12, and edges on p10 and p12 picked real cards, so they were set aside. The claims of a heavy dark outline are mostly not visible on the cards. The moon base is the weakest card and needs the biggest edit.

1. **Invented ground under the hero. Lumpy grey clay mounds (rover, telescope), a wide crater plain full of pebbles with twinkle stars (moon base) and a riveted brushed-steel floor (console). None of the real cards stand an object on fake terrain. They use one real material (road, planks, paving, pool) or a plain graphic field. This is the strongest tell in the set, and staging, edges and value all describe it.** Stand the object on one plain, real surface such as wooden planks, a dusty road or a smooth ledge, or float it on the graphic background with no ground at all. Keep the ground quiet, with a few large soft shapes.
2. **Palette narrows to one family or to neutral grey. The console is bare grey steel on grey steel, the moon base card is lavender everywhere, and the helmet clashes magenta wall with violet ledge. Real cards give the hero a clear local colour against a background of a different family.** Give the hero a clear painted local colour, set it against one calm background colour from a different family, and let shadows go to a deeper version of the local colour.
3. **Every surface carries the same lacquer gloss, plus evenly scattered small decoration: rivets, dimples, pits, speckle. Real cards are mostly soft matte, with gloss on one or two parts.** Use soft matte paint for the main body, keep gloss only on glass or one accent part, and use a few large, soft details in place of many small ones.
4. **A bright glow sits right behind the hero on sunburst and ring backgrounds (rocket, UFO). It is a mild tell, and both of those cards are close to passing.** Keep sunbursts and rings at an even, tone-on-tone brightness behind the object.
5. **Dark accents go to solid black: rover tyres, asteroid crater holes. Real cards keep their darks coloured and rounded.** Paint the darkest parts as deep coloured greys with a soft highlight on the rounded edge.

- p01: Paint the console body a soft teal painted metal and keep the buttons distinct red, yellow and green. Replace the riveted steel floor with plain, slightly darker grey-blue tiles.
- p02: keep
- p03: keep
- p04: Stand the rover on a flat dusty beige road in place of the grey mounds. Make the tyres dark slate grey with a soft lit upper rim. Make the orange body slightly more matte.
- p05: keep
- p06: Make the yellow rings an even, flat tone-on-tone with no bright centre behind the saucer. Give the teal hull a slightly softer satin finish, with the gloss kept to the green dome.
- p07: Paint the rock a slightly warmer clear brown-violet with a few large smooth craters instead of fine pits. Make the crater interiors deep brown, not black.
- p08: Paint the ground a warm grey-beige with only two or three large soft craters and no scattered pebbles. Make the sky a plain deep blue with no sparkle stars. Make the habitat clean matte white with one coloured trim.
- p09: Stand the tripod on plain wooden floorboards in place of the grey mounds and crater. Make the tube slightly cooler, a light warm-white instead of cream.
- p11: Make the wall and ledge one calm colour family, such as a soft lilac wall on a slightly darker lilac ledge. Make the blue and white shell matte painted plastic, with the gloss kept to the visor.
