## Art director

Yes, this is close. Three cards pass as they are (pumpkin patch, house, crystal ball), and two need only small value and backdrop touches (candelabra, jack-o-lantern). The rendering language, saturation and subject appeal already match the real cards. The bat is furthest off because of its plush fur. The coffin is next, with a mismatched floor and everything in one orange, followed by the broom, tombstone and cauldron. All of these can be fixed with colour, light and finish.

Specialist calls I overruled:
- Edges and linework named the generated card correctly on only one of the ten test pairs. It picked the real card on eight, so its 'less detail, softer edges' advice is dropped. Real cards carry more crisp detail than these do, not less.
- The surface report's general call to reduce detail is dropped for the same reason. Its picks of the real topiary, door, vending machine and towel are overruled.
- Control-pair flags on the lion, towel and coral were false alarms on real cards, so an out-of-range number alone did not justify an edit.

Excluded by the brief: all proportion, thickness and roundness fixes, plus the crop, turn and tilt requests from the composition and viewpoint reports. Those reports are used only where the fix sits in the backdrop, such as the coffin's floor.

1. **Heroes have no committed light. Form turns in a slow, even gradient with no clear lit side, no broad dark shadow shape and no dark accent at the contact point. The bat is the worst case, reading as one dark mass, and the tombstone, coffin and broom show it too. Merges the lighting and value reports, which describe the same fault.** Light the object from the upper left. Give it one bright lit side, one broad dark shadow shape on the lower right, and a small near-black accent where it touches the ground. Applies to p03, p07, p09 and p10, and slightly to p05 and p11.
2. **The bat's felt fuzz is a foreign material. It reads as a photographed plush toy, and no real card has that surface. The broom's lacquered shine on every straw and the cauldron's scratchy grain are smaller cases of the same fault. This is limited to these cards. The surface report's general 'too much fine detail' pattern is overruled: the real topiary, ivy door, sneaker mesh and terry towel are all dense and crisp, and the surface report picked real cards on p01, p06, p08 and p12.** Paint the bat as smooth soft shapes with one broad sheen. Make the broom straw matte and dry. Make the cauldron iron clean and smooth with soft gradients. Leave the detail level on all other cards as it is.
3. **Hero and setting fall into one hue family, so hue roles collapse. The coffin and floor are the same orange wood. The broom, handle and floor are all orange. The tombstone is the same pale value as its sky. The cauldron is the reverse fault: its grey ledge has nothing to do with the magenta wall. Real cards give hero, wall and floor distinct roles, and real ledges share the wall's hue.** Give floor and object different colours. Use a muted grey-tan floor under orange wood, a clearly deeper blue sky behind the pale stone, and a ledge tinted from the same hue as the wall.
4. **On the coffin, the floor and the object use two different cameras. The boards run dead horizontal while the coffin is seen three-quarter from above. This is the clearest single error on any card. It can be fixed in the floor alone, without touching the coffin.** Run the floor boards back toward the wall in gentle perspective, with thin soft seams, as on the real plank floors.
5. **Some backdrops sit at the wrong level against the hero. The jack-o-lantern's violet rays are as loud as the pumpkin, above the real chroma range. The candelabra's cyan diamonds are nearly one flat bright level, the flattest card in the set.** Make the violet rays slightly dustier and deeper toward the corners. Deepen the darker diamonds slightly to a medium teal.
6. **Glows are self-lit and do not touch nearby surfaces, as in the jack-o-lantern's carved face and the candle flames. This is a minor fault. On the house it is mild enough to leave alone.** Let the glow fade from bright yellow to dark orange across the carved walls. Let the flames warm the top of the wax slightly.

- p01: keep
- p03: Repaint the bat as a smooth painted surface with one broad soft sheen on the head and upper body, and only a slightly fuzzy edge. Light it from the upper left: lighten the top of the head and the left wing to a mid dusty purple, and let the lower right fall into deep blue-violet shadow. Make the yellow rings slightly fainter.
- p04: Tint the ledge a lighter, dustier version of the magenta wall so wall and ledge are one colour family. Colour the cauldron iron a deep blue-violet with rich indigo shadows and a broader mid-toned lit side. Keep the metal smooth with clean gradients and one soft highlight on the shoulder.
- p05: Slightly deepen the darker diamonds to a medium teal. Add dark brown shadow bands on the undersides of the arms and along the stem, and let the flames warm the top of the wax slightly.
- p06: keep
- p07: Deepen the sky stripes to a clearly richer blue so the stone stands apart from them. Make the stone slightly darker and cooler, with a firm shadow down its right side and a deep dark inside the carved cross. Paint the moss as smooth soft green shapes and quiet the fine grain on the stone.
- p08: keep
- p09: Make the bristles a matte dry golden straw with no shine, with deep shadow between the clumps and under the red binding. Change the floor to a muted grey-brown wood with thin faint seams, so straw and floor separate. Darken the handle to a reddish brown and make the cast shadow a deeper cool purple-brown.
- p10: Run the floor boards back toward the wall in gentle perspective, in a pale greyed tan with thin soft seams. Make the coffin a darker, cooler red-brown with a few long soft grain lines and a bright near corner. Add a dark contact line along the base and a cool violet-brown cast shadow on the floor.
- p11: Make the purple rays slightly dustier and deeper toward the corners. Fade the glow from bright yellow to dark orange across the carved walls, with near-black accents at the deepest cuts. Slightly deepen the lower-right shadow side with cool red-brown.
