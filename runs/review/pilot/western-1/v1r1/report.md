## Art director

Close, but not there across the set. Of the 10 generated cards, the wagon (p10) and barrel (p06) could pass as they are. The star, hat and cactus are near and need only slight colour or value changes. The boot, saloon and windmill are noticeably off through surface or colour and lighting. The coffee pot (p07) and wanted poster (p08) are the clear misses, because they read as photographic. The main fault is literal surface detail, then dull or tinted colour fields, then flat lighting on scene cards. The form language is already right.

Overruled: shape_proportions asked to thin and slim forms, but real cards here are just as plump (jacket, locker, signpost, bumper car), so that would over-correct toward realism; the boot straps, wagon canopy, cactus and windmill timbers keep their weight. Staging_detail and composition_hierarchy treated extra props and split focus as tells, but the butterfly, ivy, life ring, pier and lily pads are all on real cards, so those fixes are dropped. Surface_finish, edges_linework and color_palette also flagged real cards (door, tennis ball, racket strings, sneakers, suitcase), and the real phone booth and bumper car drew calls too. I used those specialists only where the fault is visible on a generated card. Cast-shadow requests for floating objects are dropped, because the real umbrella and flippers have none. On p06 the warm-share breach is visible but not enough to redraw a card that passes; on p10 the slightly bright canvas and warm cast likewise do not justify an edit.

1. **Literal material and wear detail. The worst cards drop out of the game's smooth, plump 3D look into photo or printed surfaces: enamel specks, rust chips and a dent on the coffee pot, paper fibres and creases on the poster, dashed stitching and leather grain on the boot, dense plank lines on the saloon. Worst on p07 and p08, then p02 and p09. Surface_finish, edges_linework and staging_detail describe this one problem from different sides.** Paint each material as a smooth, softly blended surface with a few large shapes and one broad soft highlight. Show material through matte or glossy finish, using a few large marks at most.
2. **Backdrops and grounds carry too much surface or too hard a pattern. The rock slab on p07, knotted boards on p08 and sharp diamond grid on p02 compete with the hero. Real cards do use wallpaper motifs, rings, rays and plank floors, but those stay soft and quiet.** Build the background from a few large, soft, low-contrast shapes that stay quieter than the hero. Keep any pattern tone-on-tone with soft edges.
3. **Colour casts and dull fields. Peach covers all of p09, pink covers p11, and olive and grey leave p08 almost colourless. The dark, dull backgrounds on p03 and p01 are a milder form of the same fault. Real cards use a clean, bright field that sets a clear colour split against the hero.** Use one bright, clean background colour that contrasts with the hero's colour, with light outer areas and clear blue skies on outdoor scenes. Keep neutrals and metals free of the background tint.
4. **Scene cards read as flat storybook painting rather than lit 3D objects. On p09 and p11 the hero sits in even light, with faces close in tone and shape built from thin dark lines instead of large light and shadow masses. p05 has a milder version, with hero and wall in similar mid values.** Light the hero from one side so it has a bright side and a clearly darker, cool shaded side, grouped into a few large value masses with a soft ground shadow.

- p01: Lift the background slightly to a brighter pink-magenta with lighter outer rings. Let the star's right and lower arms fall slightly darker.
- p02: Change the background to soft tone-on-tone blue rings with blurred edges. Draw the shaft swirls as a few smooth raised pale shapes, with plain seams. Paint the leather smooth with one broad soft highlight.
- p03: Make the teal background slightly brighter and cleaner, with lighter corners. Keep the hat as it is.
- p05: Make the pink stripes slightly brighter and cleaner. Paint the sand as smooth soft dunes. Deepen the cactus's shaded side slightly and blur the edge of its cast shadow.
- p06: keep
- p07: Paint the pot as clean, smooth, glossy blue enamel with an unbroken wall, clean rims and a few larger soft pale dots. Make its shapes slightly plumper. Replace the rock with a smooth stone ledge made of a few large soft shapes.
- p08: Paint the poster as a thick, smooth cream sheet with soft rounded edges and two or three broad gentle folds. Make the upper wall a bright, clean turquoise. Paint the lower wall as smooth warm-brown boards with a few wide gaps.
- p09: Make the sky a clear pale blue and the ground a lighter, cooler sandy beige. Paint the walls with fewer, wider, smooth planks. Light the saloon from the upper left, with the right side wall in clearly darker cool shade.
- p10: keep
- p11: Make the sky a clear bright blue with pale clouds and the ground a sandy tan. Paint the blades and tank a clean cool grey. Use a low side sun so each tower leg has a bright face and a dark face, with a soft shadow on the ground.
