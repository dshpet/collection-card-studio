## Art director

Yes, close. All ten generated cards read as the same family at a glance, and the hat passes as it is. The star and boot need only small touches. The remaining tells are specific to each card, not a style failure. The main ones are all-orange warm cards where hero and backdrop share a hue, added realistic wear and fine surface marks, the train's hard black lacquer, and busy secondary staging. The specialists were often split or low confidence, and several picked real cards as fake, which also suggests the set is close. Use light edits only, because the risk now is over-correcting.

1. **Warm cards collapse into one orange family. Hero, support and backdrop share a hue, so the hero has no color separation. The real cards mostly set the hero against a contrasting field: navy on cyan, tan on green, blue on orange. Worst on the saddle and the boot, then the saloon. The lasso has it in the rope and wood.** Set a warm hero on a cool blue or teal backdrop. Make wood and rope duller and less orange, and keep one strong color family on the object.
2. **Fine surface wear and marks are added at one even sharpness: chips, rust and scratches on the pot, dashed stitching and pore grain on the saddle, dense plank lines on the saloon, weave on the canvas, rivets on the train. This reads as a more realistic finish than the soft, quiet real materials. Surface and edge reports describe the same fault.** Paint materials as smooth, clean and soft, with one broad gentle highlight and a few faint marks. Save the crispest detail for one focal spot.
3. **The train has a hard lacquer finish: near-black body, sharp white streaks and polished gold everywhere. The star's silver has a smaller version, a pearly speckled sheen. Real vehicles such as the bus have soft satin paint, and real darks keep some color.** Lift black to dark blue-charcoal with a soft satin sheen. Use fewer, softer highlights. Keep metal clean and smooth.
4. **Staging is busier than the real cards around the hero: fence posts around the saddle, post and brace beside the rope, pebbles and grass tufts on the wagon ground, stones and a bright fire around the pot, and hard high-contrast stripes behind the wagon. The second element competes with the hero. Overruled as a general rule: the real signpost has a butterfly and the real jellyfish has bubbles, so a single charming extra is allowed. The fault is competing mass and scattered small bits.** Keep one simple support and a smooth ground. Use two close tones for stripes. Let the hero be the brightest and strongest shape.
5. **The cactus is flat in value. Its green, pink and sand sit at similar greys, the light is even, and only the cast shadow goes dark, while the green is louder than a believable plant. The real cards hold a clear dark side. This is specific to this card, and evenly lit shading is not a rule across the set: the real wheel is also evenly lit.** Give the object a clear darker shadow side that matches its cast shadow, and add deep tones in recesses.
6. **Overruled. Several reports picked a real card as the fake, so their reasons do not apply. Thin rays and dark corners appear on the real jacket. A glow behind the hero appears on the real ice tray. Droplets on glass are on the real vending machine. A flat graphic look is on the real ticket, and carved wood grain on the real signpost. A dark low-key field and a glowing rim are on the real jellyfish. The shape report's inflated-form pattern is also dropped, because form cannot be judged across objects, so the swollen-form and toy-proportion fixes are not used. The viewpoint report's camera notes are not acted on either: they were low confidence or concerned form, and they are outside what the notes may change.** No change: leave rays, background glow, and the hero's form and camera as they are.
