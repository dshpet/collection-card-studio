## Art director

Yes, this is close. Three of the ten generated cards pass as they are (hat, star badge, cactus), and the saloon needs only a slight colour change. The other six need small fixes to extras, colour or light, and only the locomotive stands out strongly.

I overruled a lot, because the specialists' main style pattern points at real cards. Their complaints about inflated form, speckled frost, busy paving, heavy contours, noisy surfaces and dark corners with a glow all landed on real cards: jacket, pier, ice tray, sneakers, vending machine and fountain. Inflation complaints are also excluded by the form rule. Real cards also carry pebbles, thin rays and whole, centred heroes, so none of those are tells. Lighting calls on the fountain and pier were wrong for the same reason, and the two controls drew several false alarms.

Three real tells remain: invented props that break the staging, warm heroes on warm grounds, and the locomotive's hard black-and-glint finish. A fourth, camera disagreement between parts of an object, was the most reliable signal, with nine correct calls and none wrong. It is ranked as an issue but left out of card notes because it falls outside the allowed changes, so boot, wagon, train and lasso keep that flaw after their edits.

1. **An added prop breaks the staging. The saddle rail cuts the flat wave field in two and a floor shadow sits under the stirrup on empty air. The campfire is the brightest spot on the pot card. The fence outweighs the thin rope. Real cards in these pairs use one plain surface or a clean float.** Show the hero alone. It floats on the pattern with no shadow beneath it, or it stands on one plain surface with one soft contact shadow.
2. **Hero and ground share one warm family, and shadows stay orange. The boot sits on orange rings, the wagon wood on orange ground, the saddle on a matching orange rail, and the saloon walls on tan sand. This is the clearest colour tell, and the warm-share readings support it on boot and saloon.** Set warm leather and wood against a cool or pale dusty ground. Use a browner, slightly greyed wood or leather, with cool plum-brown shadows.
3. **The locomotive has the hardest local contrast in the set: near-black paint, sharp white and gold glints on every edge, and even light across all parts. Four specialists describe this one problem, and it is out of range on contrast, detail and gloss. The real cards, such as the bus, group this into one lit side and one shadow side.** Paint black parts as deep blue-charcoal satin. Use a few soft highlights and one broad shadow side.
4. **Parts of one object disagree on the camera. The boot shows its inner back wall and also its sole. The wagon arch and side wheels are both near-frontal. The train funnel shows its top above a mid-boiler horizon. The lasso loop, coil and beam each tilt differently. This specialist made nine calls, all correct, but the cause is subtle at card size. It sits outside the note limits, so it goes in no card note.** Draw every part of the object from one camera height and one mild three-quarter view.
