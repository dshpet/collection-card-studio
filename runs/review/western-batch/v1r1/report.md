## Art director

This batch is close to the real cards. The bandana, campfire scene and lasso can stay, and the boot and cactus need only light touches. The remaining tells are about staging and light, not style: ground scattered with pebbles, tufts and shrubs; front lighting with no darker side (hat, mesa, cactus); and a hero whose colour is washed out or sinks into the background (windmill, horseshoe, mesa). Several specialists repeatedly picked the real card, and I overrode those findings: form and outline calls, the 'make everything smooth' push, and the claim against night scenes. The real cards carry plenty of fine detail, so the edits only remove clutter and set the lighting and colour roles, and add no smoothing.

1. **Scattered small ground clutter. Pebbles, a stray rock, grass tufts and shrubs dot the ground on the wagon, cactus, mesa, horseshoe and campfire cards, and the windmill's field is made of separate blade strokes. The real cards (coaster, popcorn cart, sneakers, topiary lawn) keep the ground as broad, smooth, even areas. This is the most common tell, and earlier kept edits (Moon Rover, Pig Roper, Wagon) fixed exactly this.** Paint the ground as one smooth, even surface (dirt, sand or field) in broad soft tones. Leave the space around the hero clear.
2. **Flat front lighting. On the cowboy hat, the mesa and the cactus, both sides of the hero are almost equally bright, and the form rounds only through a centre-bright gradient. The real cards (binoculars, jacket, topiary, lighthouse) use one side key light and let the far side fall into a clearly darker shade with a cast shadow.** Light the hero from the upper left so its right side is clearly darker, and cast a soft shadow onto the ground to the lower right.
3. **The hero colour is washed out or sinks into the ground colour. The windmill is nearly colourless cream and grey, the horseshoe is dull grey between hot pink and orange, and the mesa is orange-red on orange sand. The real cards give the hero one clear, saturated local colour that stands apart from its backdrop (red and white lighthouse, yellow coaster cars, red popcorn cart).** Give the hero one clean, saturated local colour, with a backdrop in a different hue family.
4. **Fine dashed stitching and grain. This shows on the boot only. I overrule the general 'make everything smooth' push from surface_finish and edges_linework: the real cards carry plenty of fine detail (the topiary's separate leaves, the bird feeder's plank grain and twisted rope, the popsicle's frost droplets, the popcorn heap). Only the dashed stitch lines are a confirmed tell, and the Cowboy Boots edit that fixed them was kept.** Card-level only. Change dashed stitching to a few soft solid seam lines, and keep the rest of the detail.
5. **Overruled findings. Shape_proportions, edges_linework (outline darkness) and composition picked the real card on most pairs: the neckerchief, coaster, motel sign and jacket are all real. Value_contrast's claim that no real card uses a dark night scene is wrong, because the motel sign is real. Form, pose, frontal symmetry and thin silhouettes (lasso, hat, horseshoe) are visible tells, but the rules don't let edits change form, so these are left alone.** No action: do not change form, pose or framing in the edits.
