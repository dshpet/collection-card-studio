## Art director

These are close to the real cards. The specialists often picked the wrong card. The cutting board was flagged by most specialists. The toaster and the pot were each caught by fewer than half, and real cards (neck pillow, music player, lion, vending machine) were picked just as often. What gives the generated cards away is finish and contrast, not form. The materials look photographic and glossy, with extras like droplets and rivets. The backdrop or a light source holds the strongest contrast. In p03, the hero is too small. Each card gets two or three small edits to finish, background value and framing. Their objects, colors and staging stay as they are.

1. **Photographic, glossy material finishes with added micro-detail. The toaster has mirror-like studio chrome. The pot is lacquered enamel scattered with water droplets, with riveted handles. The board has fine streaky grain over its whole surface and a woven texture in the cloth. Real cards (sunscreen, neck pillow, vending machine, music player) use broad satin surfaces with one or two soft highlights.** Paint metal and enamel as soft satin with one broad soft highlight and a slightly darker side. Keep grain and weave faint and wide. Draw droplets only where the subject is wet food.
2. **The backdrop carries the strongest contrast or light on the card. B's gingham in p03 has full-contrast white and dark checks, and the board almost disappears into it. The orange flame glow in p05 spreads across a near-black stovetop. In p02, a white hotspot on the floor flattens the chrome toaster into the backdrop. The real cards keep their backdrop patterns tone-on-tone (green waves, pink and violet rings), so the hero wins on value.** Keep the backdrop to a few quiet tones close in value, and make the hero's lit and shadow sides the strongest light-against-dark on the card. Keep glows small and local.
3. **The hero is small inside a busy backdrop (p03 board, occupancy 0.061). Real cards crop close so the hero fills most of the card.** Bring the camera closer so the hero fills most of the card and reaches near the edges.
4. **Overruled. Several specialists picked a real card as generated in the controls. In p01 they chose the neck pillow, and in p04 the music player or the lion. Those real cards show that puffy rounded forms, saturated concentric-ring backdrops, a fully detailed park scene and a frontal floating hero all belong to this game. Shape and proportion complaints are out of scope and were dropped. Other calls that hit a real card were also discarded: the vending machine (viewpoint, palette, finish), the lion's park and marble (staging, edges, value, palette), the bird feeder's contour, and the 'crack' veins on the lion.** Make no rule changes to form, ring or wave backdrops, outdoor scene density, or saturation level. They match the real cards.

- p02: Toaster: Make the chrome a soft satin with one broad highlight down the left of the front and a slightly darker right side, in place of the mirror reflections. Tint the floor under the toaster to the same mid pink as the wall, with no white hotspot. Keep the toast, the red knob and the lever.
- p03: Cutting Board: Make the tablecloth a soft pale blue with faint, low-contrast light blue checks, with no pure white squares and no weave texture. Bring the camera slightly closer so the board fills more of the card. Make the board's wood smooth with a few faint wide grain lines, and keep the tomato, cucumber and carrot slices.
- p05: Cooking Pot: Remove the water droplets from the lid and pot, and give the teal enamel one soft broad highlight. Keep the flame glow small and right under the pot, and make the stovetop a slightly lighter neutral dark grey with no orange spill. Keep the tiles, the steam and the handles.
