## Art director

Close, but not there yet. The rendering family is right, and the whistle passed all nine specialists, so it stays. The other seven are recognisably game-like, but their inflated toy forms and simplified surfaces give them away, and the current style text directly asks for both ('big smooth forms', 'few bold marks'). Barrel, taxi and tent are furthest off. Many of the specialists' halo, vignette, speckle and warm-neutral calls pointed at the real cards, so I overruled them. The fix is leaner shapes and more fine material detail, not more simplification.

1. **Objects are inflated. Tubes, cords, rims, hoops, bumpers and posts swell, and tops and bases go soft and domed. Straight runs and tight corners turn into bumps. This is the strongest tell (bugle, basket, barrel, taxi, tent, cauldron) and the current text causes it with 'few big smooth forms' and 'gently rounded edges'.** Draw lean, true real-world proportions: flat faces, straight edges, tight corners, thin rims, hoops and cords, and small accessories at true scale.
2. **Material finish is too simple. Generated surfaces are smooth lacquered toy plastic or carry big decorative marks: raised flower on the cup, thick glossy rim, uniform apples with identical highlights, swooshes on the barrel staves. The real cards have more fine detail: thin grain lines, flecked apple skin, separate rope strands, flat painted decoration, thin trim, see-through windows. The 'few bold marks instead of fine grain' rule causes this. I overrule surface_finish and edges_linework on p02 and p08: the crisp rope strands and fringed tassels on the bugle and the speckled soot on the pot are on the real cards, so they are not tells. I also overrule their advice to simplify detail; it would move the generator further from the real cards.** Paint fine real material: thin grain lines along wood, twisted rope strands, natural skin flecks on fruit, patterns painted flat on the surface, thin trim.
3. **Invented or oversized extras: a leaf on every apple, chunky log stakes and tiebacks, overrider bumpers, rope lashings, a heavy stone ring, large flowers. They add bulk and make the card look staged.** Draw only the plain parts of the object: plain apples with short stems, thin pegs, a slim bumper, thin posts.
4. **Camera sits too close and too high on some cards. The taxi nose and near wheel swell, the barrel shows its open top, and the deck rail bends into a round platform. Some subjects also sit neatly contained and centred, while real cards are larger or run off an edge. I overrule composition's request to make the bugle bigger and chunkier: the real bugle is the thin one.** Use mild perspective from a distant camera with straight ground lines and the object large in frame, free to touch a card edge.
5. **Local colour is pushed toward one saturated hue: barrel wood goes pumpkin orange and taxi paint goes amber. I overrule color_palette's general claim that warm neutrals and cream whites are a tell: the real cup is the warm cream one with greyed blue, and the real pot is warm grey with a red glow. Cool lilac shadows and pure blue on p03 belong to the generated card.** Keep natural local colours: tan-brown wood, clean yellow paint, softly greyed decoration on ceramics.
6. **Background and halo claims are overruled as a style rule. The pale halo and bleached field called out on p01 are on the real card, and the real bugle has the hard pale rays. The real cup has the dark vignette and the real tent has the black doorway and dark corners are on the generated side of no consistent rule. Specialists disagree with the key here, so no glow, vignette or key rule goes into the style text. Backgrounds differ by category and should follow the references. Halo notes stay only as one-card fixes where the generated card has one (taxi).** Follow the attached references for background. Fix glow only on the individual card.
7. **Lighting signals were mostly unreliable. On p01 the deep broad shadow side belongs to the generated card, and the frontal, evenly lit body belongs to the real one, while on p05 the real barrel has the better side turn. The 'deep, saturated shadow side' wording is too strong, so I am softening it. Lighter fix: firm contact and a soft, short cast shadow where generated shadows run long and hard (basket).** One clear side light turning smoothly into shadow, with one soft highlight on shiny materials.

Style text:

The attached images are real cards from the game. Draw one new card that could sit beside them unnoticed: the same rendering, lighting, finish, color and level of detail.
Draw the object with lean, true real-world proportions: flat faces, straight edges, tight corners, thin rims and cords, small accessories.
One clear side light turning smoothly into shadow; shiny materials carry one soft highlight.
Paint fine real material: thin grain lines, twisted rope strands, flat painted patterns, natural local colors.
Use mild perspective from a distant camera, straight ground lines, object large in frame.
Draw only what the subject names. No text, letters, numbers, frame, border or watermark.
