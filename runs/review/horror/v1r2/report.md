## Art director

Close, but not there yet. Backdrops, palette, saturation and overall rendering sit well beside the real cards, and most pairs are low-confidence calls. The pumpkin and witch hat could pass now, and the candelabra needs only a backdrop touch. The gravestone is the furthest off because it reads as photographed stone. The house is next, then the coffin and the cat. The root causes are surface texture layers, invented extras and even frontal light, and all can be fixed with edits.

Overruled:
- All proportion, thickness and viewpoint fixes, because form cannot be judged across different objects.
- All framing fixes such as bigger, closer or cropped, because framing is outside the permitted note changes.
- The call against the real lion on p08, which is a control.
- Calls against the real towel, gloves, duck, scarf, drink and shoes, none of which is generated.
- The grey-brown table for the pumpkin, because the real glove card uses equally warm wood.
- Near-black roofs on the house, because the roofs are mid-tone purple on the card.

1. **An even texture layer sits over the hero at one mark size, unlike the broad smooth planes of the real cards: photo-like pitted stone on the gravestone, felt fuzz on the cat, fine scratches on the coffin, and a tile or stone mark on every part of the house.** Paint surfaces as large smooth planes with a soft satin sheen. Show material with a few long, simple marks, grouped around the focus, and leave wide areas plain.
2. **Invented story extras sit around the hero: a daylight moon as the brightest shape on the house card, headstones beside the coffin, moss and a leaf on the gravestone, and bubbles floating above the cauldron. The real cards here show one object and its plain setting.** Show the object alone in its setting. Fill the space behind it with calm sky, soft trees or the plain backdrop.
3. **Light on the hero is frontal and even, and the darks fall to flat near-black. The house walls match on both sides, while the real lighthouse puts a whole side in cool shadow. The cat and the coffin side lose their color in shadow.** Light each hero from one side. Let the far side fall into a deep cool shadow that keeps its color, navy or plum or red-brown, and step through soft middle tones to reach it.
4. **Some heroes have one airbrushed finish with blurred highlights, and materials lose their identity: the crystal ball is a soft smear, the cauldron iron reads as lacquered plastic, and the broom straw is smooth and has no fibre.** Give each material its own finish: crisp thin reflections on glass and polished metal, a dry matte surface on iron and straw, and fine lines that follow the form.
5. **Graphic backdrops are softer and paler than the real ones. The candelabra rays and broom waves wash out, and the gravestone wall is one flat fill. Real fields have crisp tonal steps and a soft gradient. Fire glow on the cauldron's floor is a smaller case of the same softness.** Draw rays, waves and rings with crisp edges and clear steps of one hue, lighter at the centre and darker in the corners. Give plain walls a soft light-to-dark gradient.

- p02: Make the purple sunburst rays slightly crisper, with clearer steps from light to deep purple. Remove the pale patch behind the stem.
- p03: keep
- p04: Replace the fine fuzzy grain with smooth, softly shaded fur. Paint the cat deep blue-charcoal with cool blue sheen on the head and back, lit from the upper left, with the right side in soft navy shadow. Slightly shorten and soften the floor shadow.
- p05: keep
- p06: Remove the moon and show blue sky with soft white clouds there. Merge the roof tiles and wall stones into larger smooth areas with only a few thin joints. Light the house from the right: right walls warm and bright, left walls in deep cool blue-violet shadow.
- p07: Give the iron a slightly matte cast finish with a soft broad light on the belly and a dull narrow highlight on the rim. Keep the bubbles inside the pot and remove those floating in the air. Slightly reduce the orange glow on the stone floor.
- p09: Give the straw a dry matte finish with fine fibre lines along each bunch and dark shading between bunches. Make the handle a slightly deeper brown. Give the wave backdrop clearer steps from light blue to deep blue, darker toward the corners.
- p10: Remove the two headstones and show large soft trees and bushes in their place. Replace the dense scratchy grain with a few long soft grain lines and a satin sheen on the lid. Lift the side wall from near-black to a rich red-brown, with plum in the deepest shadow.
- p11: Give the mist inside the ball defined swirling shapes with visible soft edges. Add one crisp curved window reflection and a sharp bright rim light on the upper left. Deepen the blue toward the lower right edge, keeping the centre light.
- p12: Paint the stone as smooth, simple planes of cool blue-grey with one thin clean crack and a light satin sheen on the top edge. Remove the moss and the leaf. Add a soft light-to-dark gradient to the orange wall.
