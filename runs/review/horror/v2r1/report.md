## Art director

Yes, this is close, and only three cards still clearly read as not from this game. The pumpkin pail, spell book and potion could pass, so I kept them. The bat, chest, hearse, castle and tree each need one small fix to colour, light or extras. The skull and web are furthest off. The skull is grey clay and the web is colourless tubing, and the tree's moon and leaf are also an obvious giveaway.

I overruled a lot of specialist calls because the answer key contradicts them:
- Controls: the real flower bed (p11 B) was flagged by six specialists, for glossy petals, swollen stones and dark contours. The real map (p03 A) was flagged by five, one of them at confidence 4.
- The real door (p12 A): four specialists picked it, one at confidence 4, for busy ivy, gloss and heavy lines. The real topiary (p10 B) and shoes (p07 B) drew similar calls.

Real cards are glossier, busier and more strongly outlined than the reports assume, so I dropped the heavy-contour, too-busy and too-glossy calls as tells. The shape, camera and framing fixes are out of scope and are not in the notes. The remaining pattern holds up on the cards: neutral heroes, even light with weak coloured shadows, and invented extras.

1. **Some heroes lose their own colour and fall to plain grey or black in shade. The real cards keep rich colour in every shadow, such as the red-orange hull of the boat and the blue-violet of the shoes. The skull is grey on grey, the hearse is dead neutral black, the web is white-grey on pink, and the tree's branches turn cold grey on an orange trunk. This is the strongest giveaway.** Give the hero a clear colour of its own from light to dark. Tint whites toward warm ivory with violet-blue shadows. Tint blacks toward deep blue-violet with plum shadows. Paint one object in one colour family from end to end.
2. **Light comes from the front and wraps around the object, so there is no committed shadow side, and cast shadows are weak or dull. Real cards push one side into deep colour and lay a saturated shadow on the ground, like the red shadow under the ice tray and the beach ball. The castle, web, tree and skull show this most.** Light from the upper left. Put the far side of the object in deep, saturated, cool shadow. Lay one soft, richly coloured shadow on the ground, starting at the base and falling away from the light.
3. **Generated cards add things nobody asked for, and these things pull the eye: the moon and single orange leaf on the tree, the green glow along the chest lid, the cracked flagstones, and the small bushes on the hearse hills. Real backdrops are simple fields or one surface, but the busy real door shows that density alone is not the fault. The fault is invented extras with no job.** Show only the object and one simple setting: plain sky, plain floor, or a tone-on-tone pattern. Keep every other prop out.
4. **Finish slips away from the clean satin of the real cards in three ways: dry clay on the skull, mottled speckle on the bat, and hard plastic tubing on the web. Real cards have a smooth satin base with one or two soft highlights.** Use a smooth satin surface with a soft, broad sheen on the side facing the light. Keep the surface free of speckle and grain.
