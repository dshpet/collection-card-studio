## Art director

Close, but not there yet. Three of the ten generated cards (rocking horse, armchair, candle) could pass as they are. The other seven need small edits for light, colour and finish. On the plain-background cards the look is near the real set. The remaining gap is mostly flat frontal light, one colour flooding a card, and dead darks, and those are fixable edits. The hardest cards are the scenes: the gate and the cabin.

Answer key: p01, p04 and p11 name the object on the opposite side from the generated letter (Drink, Pool Bar and Sneakers are all on side A, key says B). I followed the letters, so the generated cards are the mirror, log cabin and candle. Two facts support that. The pool bar appears as the real side in p05, and all three of those objects suit a horror set while the labelled ones do not. I could not confirm this from run files, because access outside the sheets folder was blocked. If the labels are right instead, the notes for those three pairs are on the wrong cards.

Keeps: the rocking horse was picked only by the colour specialist. Its warm lean is real but mild, and an edit risks more than it fixes. The armchair's red and tufting read well on the card. The candle was picked only on form, which is out of scope here.

Overruled:
- All proportion, thickness and roundness calls, as instructed. That includes every shape_proportions fix.
- Environment overload (staging_detail, edges_linework): it hit the pool bar and bird feeder, and both are real. Busy scenes and hard scene edges are in the game's range.
- Added-marks and decal complaints (surface_finish) and whole-centred-object complaints (composition_hierarchy): both hit the fuel pump, which is real. The fruit-plate droplet complaint also hit a real card.
- The value specialist's black armchair: on the card it is deep crimson with visible tufting.
- The stone plinth under the mirror: built slabs and ledges also appear on real cards, such as the pump's slab.

The controls show how noisy the panel is: both real pairs, p06 and p10, drew confident odd-card calls from several specialists, and most picks in p11 went to the real sneakers. Single-specialist calls should carry little weight.

1. **Flat frontal light on generated heroes. Side planes barely darken and there is no core shadow, so round forms and walls read as flat. Real cards (binoculars, bugle, spotlight, fuel pump) keep one clear light direction, a shadow side that goes well down but stays open, and a firm cast shadow. Seen on the moon, gate, candy corn, cabin and mirror.** Light the object from one side. Make one side bright and the opposite side clearly darker, with a soft core shadow between them and a firm shadow where the object meets its surface or falls on the ground.
2. **One colour floods the card, so the hero does not separate from its ground by hue. Real cards pair the hero against a contrasting backdrop: orange drink on purple, gold bugle on cyan, orange locker on purple, blue pillow on pink. The mirror is gold on yellow and the gate is washed violet from sky to stone. The rocking horse has the same warm lean, but it passes.** Give the backdrop a colour that contrasts with the hero, in a calmer mid tone. Keep local colours separate: stone stays grey, earth stays brown, and purple stays in the sky.
3. **Dead darks and warm-drifting neutrals. Blacks turn taupe or brown and whites turn cream, while real darks carry a tint (navy spotlight, teal-green lamp, charcoal-blue eyecups). The spider and lantern frame are flat taupe-black with little colour in the shadows. The candy tip and gate stone lose their clean neutral.** Paint blacks as a cool blue-charcoal with one broad soft sheen and colour kept in the shadows. Paint whites clean, with cool grey shadows.
4. **Materials read as one moulded coat. The mirror's gold looks like soft yellow resin, where the real bugle's metal has bright streaks and dark reflections. The cabin's roof and logs share one smooth plastic finish, and the spider carries a faint even grain.** Polished metal gets narrow bright streaks and dark reflections. Wood and shingles get a dry matte surface. Smooth shells stay clean, with one soft highlight.
5. **Orange glow rim around the moon. No real hero on these sheets has a bright halo hugging its silhouette, and the two real sunburst cards sit directly on their rays. One card only.** Set the moon's edge directly on the blue rays with a clean edge.

- p01: Paint the wall in a mid-tone dusty purple, keeping the same diamond pattern, so the gold frame stands out. Make the gold polished metal, with narrow bright streaks on the scroll ridges and deep warm brown in the recesses. Light from the upper right, with the left side of the frame slightly darker and a tight dark contact shadow under the feet.
- p02: keep
- p03: Paint the spider a cool blue-charcoal, slightly lighter than now, with cool grey-blue highlights on the legs. Give the abdomen a smooth, clean semi-matte shell with one broad soft sheen across the top. Keep the deepest dark only under the body.
- p04: Bring low sun from the upper left. Drop a wide soft shadow from the roof overhang onto the front wall, and cast a clear soft shadow from the cabin across the grass. Give the logs and shingles a dry matte surface, with the logs slightly more muted brown timber.
- p05: Paint the pillars a true pale grey stone, lit on the right faces by the moon and clearly darker on the left. Cast long soft shadows from the gate bars across the path. Paint the path a natural warm earth tone, and keep the purple in the sky.
- p07: Light the candy from the upper left: a bright left side, a darker right side with a soft core shadow, and one soft sheen at the upper left. Paint the tip clean milk white with a cool grey shadow. Replace the small triangles on the wall with a plain soft diamond pattern.
- p08: Set the moon directly on the blue rays with a clean edge and no glow. Light it as a sphere from the upper left, falling to deep amber shadow along the lower right, with a dark inner wall in each crater. Shift the surface slightly toward a cooler pale ivory.
- p09: keep
- p11: keep
- p12: Paint the frame a blue-tinted dark iron, slightly lighter than now, with a broad soft sheen on the roof. Make the flame warm yellow-orange. Add soft green bounce light on the dark underside of the base.
