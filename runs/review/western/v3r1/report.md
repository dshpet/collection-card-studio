## Art director

Close, but not there yet. The hero design, palette energy and kit all sit in the same world as the real cards, and the hat and hay bale could pass today. The bandana needs only a new backdrop, and the stagecoach and cactus need small corrections. Barrel, lantern and jug are still the clearest fakes, and most specialists caught them for the same reason: over-worked wood and clay, dead darks and an all-warm palette. The horse and gold pan sit between those groups. Answer-key labels: where B is generated (p02, p03, p07, p09, p12), the label names the real card A, so I matched notes to the western objects actually in the generated slot. I overruled heavily. All shape_proportions fixes and all viewpoint and composition fixes are out of scope, because form cannot be judged across objects and framing is not an allowed note. Shape and viewpoint were also the weakest detectors, with 3 and 2 correct picks out of 10. The busy-surface theory needs limits: the highest-confidence calls hit real cards, including the p10 door with dense ivy, the p11 terry towel and the p04 viewer. The streaky wood on the p01 book, the tile grid on the p08 slide and the truss on the p07 guitar are real too. Density and range numbers therefore do not prove a fake, and notes must not simplify below what these real cards carry. The tells I kept are the ones visible on generated cards only: knots, stains, speckle, scratches, near-black darks and tinted steel.

1. **Natural materials look over-worked: knots and ring grain in wood, stains on staves, speckle on clay, scratches on metal. This is the strongest tell, and it marks the three cards most specialists caught: barrel, lantern and jug.** Paint wood, clay and metal as smooth clean surfaces with broad soft highlights. Wood has fine straight grain and a few plank seams. Bright streaky wood is fine and is on real cards, so keep it colorful and do not blank it out.
2. **Darks go dead. Hard near-black cast shadows and near-black metal, mane and legs sit beside soft shading on the object, while lit forms have too little drop into a clear shadow side.** Light from one side. Shadow sides turn to a deep, colored, slightly cool tone. Cast shadows are soft mid-tones, darkest only at the contact point.
3. **Warm pull on color. Iron, black metal and shadows drift toward orange-brown, and hero, ground and props share one warm family with no neutral area (barrel, lantern, jug, horse).** Keep steel a true cool grey. Give each warm card one paler, calmer area such as a light tan table or ground, so the hero is the only strong orange.
4. **Grounds and backdrops are chosen without the real kit in mind: cracked dirt under the barrel, a stage-plank deck under the horse, a paisley backdrop echoing the bandana's print, and a pale washed cactus key.** Use a backdrop from the game's kit: plain tone-on-tone stripes, rays, rings or diamonds, or a simple setting that suits the object, with clean smooth ground.
5. **Even trim sharpness on the stagecoach: panel lines, seat ribs and spokes all carry the same weight, so it reads flatter and more drawn than the soft moulded real vehicles. One card only.** Handle in that card's note: slightly fewer fine lines and a smooth satin finish.

- p01: keep
- p02: Barrel. Paint the staves as clean smooth honey-tan wood with fine straight grain and a soft satin sheen. Make the iron bands smooth cool grey steel with one clean soft highlight. Make the ground smooth pale dusty beige with one or two small pebbles.
- p03: Lantern. Repaint the metal slightly lighter as blue-grey steel with soft cool highlights, and let the flame throw warm light onto the inner frame. Make the tabletop smooth wood with faint straight grain and a few seams. Make the cast shadow shorter, softer and a mid-tone.
- p05: Stagecoach. Slightly simplify the panels to one thin seam each, with a smooth satin finish on the red paint and one broad soft highlight. Paint the sand slightly smoother as one even warm tone.
- p06: keep
- p07: Horse. Stand the horse on plain packed dirt or short grass in place of the wooden deck. Paint the mane, tail and lower legs deep rich brown with visible color. Add a clear sun direction: a soft satin sheen on the back and shoulder, and a soft cool shadow on the belly and far legs.
- p08: Cactus. Deepen the orange backdrop and sand slightly. Light the cactus from one side so the right edges of the trunk and arms fall into a deeper cool green shadow. Add a firm dark contact shadow where the trunk meets the sand.
- p09: Jug. Paint the clay as smooth matte terracotta with one soft highlight, and warm reflected light on the dark right flank. Make the table paler, smooth wood with faint straight grain and a few seams. Make the cast shadow soft and mid-toned.
- p11: Bandana. Replace the paisley backdrop with plain tone-on-tone blue rays or stripes. Leave the bandana as it is.
- p12: Gold Pan. Paint the pan as smooth clean grey metal with broad soft reflections and a plain inner floor. Use one thin bright highlight along the rim. Make the nuggets smooth glossy gold with soft highlights and only a few gentle planes.
