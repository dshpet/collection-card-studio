## Art director

Yes, this is close. Three of the ten generated cards pass as they are (star, horseshoe, piano), and the other seven need only small color, light or finish edits. The main remaining tell is warm, soft, evenly lit color; texture is overworked on only a few surfaces.

The specialists cannot reliably tell these cards apart. Across the ten test pairs they picked the generated card in 32 of 90 calls. On the two control pairs, 8 of 9 specialists accused a real card on p04 and all 9 did on p09.

Overruled:
- **Shape:** all notes are dropped, because form cannot be judged across different objects.
- **Viewpoint and composition:** these are out of scope for the notes. Both specialists were also weak, and composition got only 1 of 10 right.
- **Busy backgrounds and hard edges:** the claims from staging and edges fail, because the real wreath, rose arch, captain's hat and column all carry ivy, rooms, plank grain or props.
- **Extreme values:** this claim landed mostly on real cards, including the motel, scroll, column and sneakers, so I did not ask for calmer values or fewer darks.

The measured ranges are also too narrow, since real cards fall outside them on gloss, detail and contrast. Pushing cards toward quieter or simpler would be over-correction.

The name in the answer key is always card A, not the generated card. For p03, p05, p06 and p08 the generated cards are the star, windmill, tumbleweed and piano, and my notes follow that reading.

1. **Warm colors fill the whole card. Whites go cream, wood and sand go orange, and shadows stay red-orange with no cool relief. This is the clearest tell on the saloon, tumbleweed and playing cards. Warmth alone is not the fault, because the real column and captain's hat are warm too, but their shadows turn mauve or dark.** Turn shadows cool: muted grey-blue or lilac on whites and metal, muted cool brown on wood and on the ground. Paint whites clean white and weathered wood greyer brown. Keep bright orange only on light sources and accents.
2. **The light is weak and frontal, so generated cards sit in an even mid-to-light key. They have no shadow side and few deep accents, while the real motel, column, captain's hat and scroll commit to a strong light and real darks. On the harmonica a pale glow replaces this structure. This merges the lighting and value reports, but the value specialist's reading is reversed: the darks and hot lights it blamed are mostly on real cards.** Use one clear light from the upper left. Keep one side bright and let the other side fall into a cool, darker shade. Add a deep dark under the object where it meets the ground, and small dark accents in recesses.
3. **Material texture is overworked in a photographic way: blotchy rust on the cart, knots and streaks of shine on the tumbleweed twigs, grainy enamel on the coffee pot. This applies only to those surfaces. Dense leaves and flowers are fine, because the real wreath and rose arch have them.** Paint metal, enamel and wood as clean, smooth surfaces with broad soft sheens. Put wear and bright highlights in only a few places, on top edges and upper curves.

- p01: Saloon. Paint the boards in a slightly greyer, weathered brown and the wall panels in a cooler off-white. Shade the right side wall and the balcony undersides in cool blue-grey. Lower the window glow slightly so it reads as daylight.
- p02: Mine cart. Repaint the body as cleaner, smoother brown metal, with a few soft worn patches only at the corners. Let the front face go from bright on the left to darker and cooler on the right.
- p03: keep
- p05: Windmill. Deepen the sky to a richer blue at the top. Give the tower wood a slightly warmer brown with deep cool shadow sides. Add a darker contact shadow on the grass under the legs.
- p06: Tumbleweed. Paint the twigs in dry grey-brown wood with some pale bleached sticks, matte, with shine only on a few upper curves. Shade the whole ball from bright upper left to cooler, darker lower right. Paint the ground shadow in cool grey-violet.
- p07: keep
- p08: keep
- p10: Coffee pot. Make the enamel slightly smoother and cleaner, with one broad soft highlight. Keep the speckle pattern and the table as they are.
- p11: Harmonica. Deepen the background to an even golden orange with no pale glow behind the object. Narrow the white patch on the cover to a soft band of light. Let the lower front edge fall into a clear darker shade.
- p12: Playing cards. Paint the faces clean white with faint grey-blue shading and a soft gradient across each card. Add small dark contact shadows where the cards overlap. Paint the cast shadow in a muted cool brown instead of orange-red.
