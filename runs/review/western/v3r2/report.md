## Art director

Yes, this is close. Four of the ten generated cards pass as they are: pan, jug, bandana and hat. The other six need small edits, not redraws. The strongest remaining tells are monochrome warm shadows, uncommitted light, scattered pebbles and one finish across the whole object. The lantern is the most exposed card: seven of nine specialists picked it, and edges_linework gave it confidence 4.

The controls show how noisy the specialists are. Several named a "generated" card with confidence 2 or 3 on p01 and p12, where both cards are real. Many other high-confidence calls landed on real cards, including the towel's terry, the book's desk, the slide's tiles and the feeder's park.

I overruled these specialist requests:
- Toning colours down or muting them: real cards are this saturated.
- Calming backdrops, tiles, cobbles or planks in general: real cards have all of these.
- The lantern's "white-hot" pool of light: it is not on the card.
- All shape, proportion and camera or crop fixes: form cannot be judged across objects, so they are out of scope for these notes.

Pair names in the answer key name card A, not the generated card. For example, the generated card on p03 is the hay bale, not the book.

1. **Shadows on warm cards stay in the object's own hue. Horse, hay bale, barrel and stagecoach fall to plain darker orange or brown, so hero and ground merge into one warm mass. The real cards tint their shadows with a related cool colour, as on the book's plum table shadow and the bag's navy underside. The colours themselves are not the fault: the real feeder, sleeping bag and coaster are just as pure, so keep them saturated.** Keep the colours bright. Tint shadows on warm objects and warm ground with plum or violet taken from the sky or backdrop, so the shadow side is cooler than the lit side.
2. **Light on the hero is too even or does not agree with itself. The barrel darkens equally on both sides, the stagecoach panels sit at one value, the hay bale's top and front are nearly equal, and the cactus shadow falls towards its lit side. Real cards commit to one lit side, one darker side and a tight contact shadow.** Use one soft light from the upper left. Put the brightest area left of centre, a clearly darker side on the right, and a small dark contact shadow where the object meets the ground. Cast shadows fall to the right.
3. **Ground planes are dotted with small pebbles, rocks and shrubs. They appear on the hay bale, cactus, stagecoach and barrel, and on neither real ground in these sheets. The real cards use loud backdrops and busy settings, so this is about the repeated scatter only, not about detail in general.** Paint the ground as large smooth shapes in close tones, with only the hero's shadow on it.
4. **One finish covers the whole object. The stagecoach is lacquered all over, the horse has an oily satin coat, and the barrel repeats fine grain across every stave. Real cards mix matte and shiny parts within one object.** Give each material its own finish: shiny on paint and metal accents, matte on wood, hair and cloth. Keep grain broad, soft and sparse.
