## Art director

Close, but not there yet. Most heroes already sit in the game's family, and the hat and wagon could pass, so both stay as they are. The rope and saddle need one small fix each. What gives the set away is mostly around the hero: the dull teal on the doors and boot, the pale cactus backdrop, the photographic fence and the stray marks on the cup and barrel. The train is the one card where the hero itself is the problem, because its body is nearly black.

I overruled several reports. All shape and proportion fixes are dropped, as are the viewpoint and crop fixes, since those are outside what notes may change. I also overruled flags on real cards: the jacket's rays and vignette, the wheel's value and grain, the neckerchief's spiral and red, the pier's lily pads, the coaster's primaries, the book and the motel sign. Those features are in the real set, so they can't count as tells. The value specialist's 'overbright' finding is weak for the same reason: the wheel, sneakers and rose arch are about as bright. Most specialist calls were confidence 2 or 3, and the controls drew confident false picks, so I relied only on what is visible on the cards.

1. **Dull, weak backdrops. The doors and boot sit on a flat grey-teal with no glow, and the cactus sits on a washed-out pale blue. Real cards use a saturated field that is lighter behind the hero. This is the first thing that gives these cards away, even at thumbnail size.** Put the hero on a bright, saturated, clean color field with a soft glow directly behind it, lighter at the centre and slightly deeper at the corners.
2. **Stray marks on the hero that no real card has: a red dot on the cup, an orange stain and hairline scratches on the barrel, brushed streaks on the tin, and hair-fine fibres on the rope. The cup's dot and the barrel's stain are the worst, because they read as generator accidents.** Keep the hero's surface clean, with broad smooth painted forms. Show material with soft tone and one or two clean highlights.
3. **Supporting surfaces are more realistic than the hero. The saddle's fence has near-photographic grey weathered grain and knots, the door floor has ruled grey boards, and the cup's wall has hard lime and green stripes. The prop looks like it comes from a different picture than the hero.** Paint fences, floors and walls as simple, soft, warm shapes with faint tone and close colors, so the hero is the most detailed thing on the card.
4. **Hero and ground lose their color roles. The barrel and its floor share one orange, the doors and boot are a single hot orange, and the train is close to solid black against pale sand. Real cards separate local colors and keep color in their darks.** Give the hero and the ground clearly different colors. Vary the hero's wood, leather or paint from light to dark, with rich colored shadows.
5. **Some heroes have no clear shadow side, mainly the doors and barrel, which roll softly under frontal light. This is the weakest issue, because real heroes such as the wheel and jacket are also lit fairly frontally, so it only matters card by card.** Where a hero looks flat, light it from the upper left and let its right side fall into a cool, darker shadow.

- p01: keep
- p03: Slightly smooth the rope: replace the hair-fine fibre lines with soft broad shading on each twist. Keep the rope's color and the purple background.
- p04: keep
- p05: Repaint the fence as plain soft planks in warm pale wood, with only a few faint grain marks and no knots or weathering. Leave the saddle and sky as they are.
- p06: Remove the orange stain from the stave and the scratches from the iron bands. Make the floor a cooler, greyer wood, clearly different from the barrel. Let the barrel's right side fall into a deep plum-brown shadow.
- p07: Change the wall to a saturated clean turquoise gradient, lighter behind the doors. Change the floor to a plain warm ledge with no board lines. Make the door wood slightly browner with a dry satin finish and darker shadow panels.
- p08: Remove the red dot from the cup. Paint the tin as smooth pale metal with soft broad reflections instead of brushed streaks. Make the wall stripes two close shades of green.
- p09: Change the background to a bright saturated sky blue with soft wide rings and a glow behind the boot. Make the leather a slightly deeper natural saddle brown, with rich warm brown in the shadows.
- p11: Lift the locomotive body from black to a deep navy blue-grey, with a broad soft highlight along the boiler and cab. Keep true black only under the running board. Make the sand a slightly richer warm tan.
- p12: Deepen the background to a rich saturated blue, keeping the diamond pattern. Bring the sand down to a warm mid tan. Make the cactus skin waxy and mostly matte, with pale cream spines.
