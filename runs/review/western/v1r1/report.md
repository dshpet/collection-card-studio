## Art director

Close, but not there yet. In full colour the set sits near the real cards: the backdrop types, rendering and subject appeal all match. The hat and lasso could pass as they are, and the wagon needs only a small ground fix. The rest are still given away by warm colour capture, busy surfaces and a few wrong backdrops or extras. The saloon doors, barrel, train and saddle are the furthest off. The tin cup needs only its dot and backdrop fixed, and the boot and cactus mainly need the hero separated from a same-family or flat ground.

Overruled:
- The inflation and form calls are out of scope, and they also fail as evidence: the real neckerchief, sneakers, gloves and anchor were all flagged as swollen.
- The viewpoint report is dropped. It was mostly weak leans, and most of its fixes are camera or form changes rather than the look.
- The composition asks to crop, enlarge or turn heroes are dropped, because real cards are also centred and whole.
- The call that cream is a generated drift is rejected, since the real caravan and computer are cream. The wagon canvas stays.
- The even-contour ink-line idea is dropped, since the same call was made on the real neckerchief and caravan.

Report problems: the staging_detail entries for p05 and p06 are swapped, so I read each against its correct sheet. Lighting and value signals were noisy, flagging real cards as often as generated ones, so I kept only what I could see on the cards themselves.

1. **Warm capture. The hero, props and ground share one orange-brown family, shadows only get deeper orange, and a single blue area provides all the contrast. This is the strongest tell: real cards separate hero from ground by hue and let shadows drift to plum, lilac or grey. Clearest on the saloon doors, barrel, saddle and boot.** Give the hero one hue and the ground or backdrop another. Paint nearby wood pale grey-tan or put a cool colour behind a warm hero. Turn shadows on wood and ground cool plum-brown.
2. **Overworked surfaces. Dashed stitching, pebbled grain, speckle, looping wood grain, scratches, a stain and rows of rivets give generated cards a busy, sharp finish. Real cards have few marks, thin seams and broad soft highlights. Seen on the saddle, boot, barrel and train.** Make materials smooth and quiet with a gentle satin sheen. Use a few thin soft seams, faint straight grain and one broad highlight.
3. **Backdrops and extras that real cards do not use: literal anchor-icon wallpaper, full-contrast stripes, a hot yellow glow behind the hero, knotty strong-grain planks, and scattered pebbles and specks. The red dot on the cup is an unexplained focal point.** Use a plain soft gradient or wide tone-on-tone abstract pattern. Draw ground as one or two large quiet shapes. Keep only props that belong to the subject.
4. **Value out of the real range on single cards. The train is a near-black mass with thin white highlights and a clipped headlamp. The cactus sits in the same mid values as its sand and wall, with no dark accent.** Lift the darkest heroes to dark charcoal-blue with wide soft sheens. Give flat cards one concentrated dark area at the contact shadow or shadow side.
5. **Some heroes are lit evenly from the front, with shading held in small creases. Real cards more often let one side fall into a broad cool shadow. This is a moderate tell: real cards were also flagged here, so apply it only where it joins the colour fix.** Light from the upper left and let the right side fall slightly into one connected cool shadow.

- p01: Change the backdrop to soft teal-blue rings of nearly one tone, with no bright glow behind the boot. Make the leather smooth with a low satin sheen and no speckle. Let the right side of the shaft and the shadow at the heel turn slightly plum.
