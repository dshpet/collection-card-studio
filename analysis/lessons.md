## Planning

- Every version keeps the topic's three to five most iconic objects, and the versions differ in the rest, in staging and in colors. *(2026-11-06: "Iconic best objects should be tried at least")*
- Each card alone must make someone name the topic. Skip ordinary objects that fit only next to the others, such as an armchair or a rocking horse in a horror set. *(2026-11-03: Armchair and Rocking Horse read as horror only beside the others)*
- Each object must be named from its picture alone, without the title, by a player from any country. Skip invented devices, such as a teleport pod, and foods or customs known in only one country, such as candy corn. *(2026-11-06: Teleport Pod 2, "I wouldn't know what it is without the title"; Candy Corn 2, "unrecognizable")*
- The audience is wide and casual, of every age and faith. Nothing morbid, sad or divisive: no death or burial objects (coffins, graves, tombstones, skulls, bones), no religious symbols (crosses, holy books, altars), no illness, injury, drugs or politics. Haunted-house fun is fine: a pumpkin, a witch hat, a friendly ghost sheet. *(2026-11-04: the coffin and the tombstone with a cross)*
- Only real animals may appear as an object. A creature with a face, such as an alien, a monster or a robot, counts as a character. *(2026-11-05: Sci-Fi planned an Alien Pet and a friendly robot)*
- Skip objects that failed in two rounds: a lasso, a horseshoe, a rocket standing on the ground and a cauldron over a fire. A rocket may fly. *(2026-11-06: Lasso 3 then 2, Horseshoe 2 and 2, grounded Rocket 2 and 2 "a toy rocket for a child", Cauldron 1 and 1 "One leg of the pot is missing")*
- The accent in hero_colors colors a part the object really has and that could be any color: "a red band on the hat", not "a red gem". Never add a part, cloth, icon or decoration to carry an accent; an object without such a part keeps its natural colors. A natural material keeps its own color: a cork stopper is light brown, a wooden handle wood brown, a rope tan. The image model draws every part the colors name, even when the subject says no. *(2026-11-05: every sheriff star got a red jewel, experiments/plain_check.py. 2026-11-06: a planet icon on the star map, "looks out of place"; stars on the boots, "Maybe remove the stars altogether"; a green cork, "the cork should be of cork colour")*
- In subject, give counts as the whole real object has them, not as one side shows them: a covered wagon has four wheels. *(2026-11-05: the two-wheeled wagon)*
- Every object has a logical structure that could stand and work in the real world. Each part named in the subject is attached to the main body or rests on the ground, and the subject says where it joins: "a ladder bolted to the hull, reaching the door at ground level." Don't plan loose or hanging accessories, such as railings, ropes, a ladder or a phone beside a booth. *(2026-11-05: "The railing is just hanging in the air unattached", "The ladder comes nowhere", "phone just floats on the outside")*
- Prefer objects with one solid, simple body. Skip objects that only look right if a complex structure is built correctly: folded paper, open frames and legs, supports and trusses, headbands with joints, suspension, or rails running into the distance. If the topic needs such an object, choose a closed, compact version of it. *(2026-11-05: map folds, waterslide, headphones, folding chair and pier failed in every style, "Chair structure is impossible")*
- Plan each object in the state it is shown in, with its working parts where they work: a flying thing has no landing feet down, a door has its handle on the side opposite its hinges, and a door or gate is seen from the side people come from, with what it leads into behind it: saloon doors from the street, the saloon inside. *(2026-11-06: Flying Saucer "Why the landing legs are shown though?"; Haunted Door "Handle placement is not logical"; Saloon Doors "They lead to where?")*
- In the subject, name the shape that makes the object recognizable, with any symmetry or regular form it must keep: "a perfectly round clock face with numbers", "a symmetrical ball". *(2026-11-05: "Shape of the clock is not completely round", "Form of the ball is wrong and not symmetrical". Naming the shape alone did not hold it: experiments/shape_check.py)*
- Put the object on the surface or in the setting where it really stands, and say so in the subject, e.g. a potted plant on grass, not on a road. *(2026-11-05: "The plant is being placed on the road, which is very strange")*
- build_model is true for a badge or emblem with a precise outline, though it looks simple. *(2026-11-05: Star Badge 2 to 4 with a Blender model; thin evidence)*
- A card may show fire, described in the subject as on the real Campfire Cook card: "a bright fire with a yellow-white glowing core, orange flame tips curling upward, glowing orange ends on the logs, and warm orange light cast on what stands near it." Never leave it as a small flame in hero_colors. *(2026-11-06: "Fire is laughable"; the tested wording, with "big", drew a bright fire 3 of 3 by eye, experiments/fire_check.py; in batch 2 this wording got 5 on the campfire and 1 on the cauldron, "Fire is off")*
- In scenes with characters, plan no action or difference in treatment that could read as harm, violence or neglect, such as kicking, hitting or one character protected while another is exposed. Gear must fit every character: if the setting needs a helmet, each one gets a helmet that fits its head, crest and ears; otherwise choose a setting that needs none. *(2026-11-06: "The guy is in helmet, but the pig isn't"; the kicked pumpkin "May look like violence")*
