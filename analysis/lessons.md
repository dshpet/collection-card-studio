# Lessons

Rules learned from the producer's grades. The planner reads the Planning rules without the cards in brackets; edit a line to teach the tool.

`scripts/experience.py propose` drafts new rules under Proposed; none is used until a person moves it up.

## Planning

- Every version keeps the topic's three to five most iconic objects, and the versions differ in the rest, in staging and in colors.
- Each card alone must make someone name the topic. Skip ordinary objects that fit only next to the others, such as an armchair or a rocking horse in a horror set. *(Armchair, Rocking Horse)*
- Each object must be named from its picture alone, without the title, by a player from any country. Skip invented devices, such as a teleport pod, and foods or customs known in only one country, such as candy corn. *(Teleport Pod 2, Candy Corn 2)*
- The audience is wide and casual, of every age and faith. Nothing morbid, sad or divisive: no death or burial objects (coffins, graves, tombstones, skulls, bones), no religious symbols (crosses, holy books, altars), no illness, injury, drugs or politics. Haunted-house fun is fine: a pumpkin, a witch hat, a friendly ghost sheet. *(Coffin, Tombstone with a cross)*
- No animals or creatures with a face as objects, real or not: they read as characters. *(Alien Pet, Robot, Bloodhound 3)*
- Skip objects that failed in two rounds: a lasso, a horseshoe, a rocket standing on the ground and a cauldron over a fire. A rocket may fly. *(Lasso 3 and 2, Horseshoe 2 and 2, grounded Rocket 2 and 2, Cauldron 1 and 1)*
- The accent in hero_colors colors a part the object really has and that could be any color: "a red band on the hat", not "a red gem". Never add a part, cloth, icon or decoration to carry an accent; an object without such a part keeps its natural colors. A natural material keeps its own color: a cork stopper is light brown, a wooden handle wood brown, a rope tan. The image model draws every part the colors name, even when the subject says no. *(Sheriff Star, Star Map, Cowboy Boots, a green cork)*
- In subject, give counts as the whole real object has them, not as one side shows them: a covered wagon has four wheels. *(two-wheeled Covered Wagon)*
- Every object has a logical structure that could stand and work in the real world. Each part named in the subject is attached to the main body or rests on the ground, and the subject says where it joins: "a ladder bolted to the hull, reaching the door at ground level." Don't plan loose or hanging accessories, such as railings, ropes, a ladder or a phone beside a booth. *(a hanging railing, a ladder to nowhere, a floating phone)*
- Prefer objects with one solid, simple body. Skip objects that only look right if a complex structure is built correctly: folded paper, open frames and legs, supports and trusses, headbands with joints, suspension, or rails running into the distance. If the topic needs such an object, choose a closed, compact version of it. *(map folds, waterslide, headphones, folding chair, pier)*
- Plan each object in the state it is shown in, with its working parts where they work: a flying thing has no landing feet down, a door has its handle on the side opposite its hinges, and a door or gate is seen from the side people come from, with what it leads into behind it: saloon doors from the street, the saloon inside. *(Flying Saucer, Haunted Door, Saloon Doors)*
- In the subject, name the shape that makes the object recognizable and the two or three signature details that make it itself: "a perfectly round clock face with numbers", "a cowboy hat with a band and a creased crown", "a boot with a spur and a stitched shaft". Name symmetry or a regular form only where the real object has it, such as a ball. *(Clock, Ball, Cowboy Hat, Cowboy Boots)*
- Put the object on the surface or in the setting where it really stands, and say so in the subject, e.g. a potted plant on grass, not on a road. *(a potted plant on a road)*
- build_model is true for a badge or emblem with a precise outline, though it looks simple. *(Star Badge 2, then 4 with a Blender model)*
- A card may show fire, described in the subject as on the real Campfire Cook card: "a bright fire with a yellow-white glowing core, orange flame tips curling upward, glowing orange ends on the logs, and warm orange light cast on what stands near it." Never leave it as a small flame in hero_colors. *(Campfire 5, Cauldron 1)*
- In scenes with characters, plan no action or difference in treatment that could read as harm, violence or neglect, such as kicking, hitting or one character protected while another is exposed. Gear must fit every character: if the setting needs a helmet, each one gets a helmet that fits its head, crest and ears; otherwise choose a setting that needs none. *(a helmet on the man but not the pig, a kicked pumpkin)*
- Plan a whole object, not part of one: a doorknob is part of a door. *(Doorknob Print 3)*
- Keep the topic's place and time: Period Drama is 1800s Europe, with no colors that read as another country, such as red and white stripes under navy. *(Hat Box)*
- Name a container by its contents, such as a hat box with a hat on the lid, and a badge by its emblem: a police shield with an eagle and a star. *(Hat Box, Badge Wallet 2)*
- A tall structure stands in its landscape, with ground across the bottom of the card: a farm windmill over wheat. Build it as the real one is built; a truss gets a Blender model. *(Windmill 3)*
- A vehicle gets its own scene, category 4. *(Covered Wagon 4)*
- The object's main body sits at the center of the card. Rings or rays go only behind an object centered on its body, not a camera with a flash at its side or a watch on a chain. *(Pocket Watch 2, Flash Camera 3)*
- A hat floats, category 1, or rests on a real surface, never on a plain floor. *(Fedora 3)*
- An object inside a clear bag shows the plastic's highlights and creases across it. *(Evidence Bag 3)*
- Name metal, porcelain and enamel matte or soft satin in hero_colors. *(Steel Safe, Handcuffs, kettle, Cake Stand, all 3)*
- A symmetric object matches on both sides, such as an even brim or an evenly opened fan, but keeps the three-quarter view. A tall object on a floor is seen from about its middle height. A handle keeps its real proportion: a magnifier's is nearly as long as the lens is wide. *(Top Hat 3, Candelabra 2, Magnifier 3 then 1)*
- On character cards the sheep keeps only its cutout's faint blush and the cow none; every leg stays clear. Two character cards of one genre do different things. *(Grand Ball 4, Hoofprint Clue 2)*
