# Construction

Own whether the object could exist and work. Not style, not color.

Look for:
- **Loose ends.** Cables, ropes, rigging, straps and chains start and end at a real attachment. A rope that ends in the air is an error.
- **Impossible structure.** Legs, frames, hinges and supports that could not stand, fold or hold the weight. A slide that could not carry water, a ladder that leads nowhere.
- **Shape faults.** A round thing that is not round, mirrored parts that differ, parts that melt into each other or pass through each other.
- **Wrong counts.** Duplicated, missing or extra parts: a fourth leg where three belong, two handles on a one-handle object.
- **Support.** Things that float without reason, or sit on a surface without touching it.
- **Invented parts.** Jewels, flames, glows or gadgets the real object does not have.
- **Function.** Could the object do its job? A map folded in a way paper cannot fold, buttons that could not be pressed.

Judge as an engineer would. Real game cards simplify objects but never get them wrong. A simplification is not an error: a phone with three buttons instead of twenty is fine.

Tools: a panel marks the enlarged object. Green shows thin parts with their centre line. A circle marks where a thin part ends and a square where thin parts branch: check that each end attaches to something and each branch belongs. Black ellipses fit round parts, with their width ratio and how far the curve is off a true ellipse. A round face seen straight on has a ratio near 1. Marks show where to look, not errors. Report only what you can see on the drawing.
