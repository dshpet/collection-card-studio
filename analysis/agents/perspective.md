# Perspective and lines

Own whether the drawing is one consistent space. Not style, not color.

Look for:
- **One camera.** Every part of the object, and the ground it stands on, is seen from the same viewpoint. A top face seen from above beside a side face seen level is an error.
- **Repeated elements follow their surface.** Keys, buttons, windows, planks, tiles and stripes shrink, tilt and bunch up as their surface turns away, and sit on rows that run parallel to that surface's edges. A grid of equal, front-facing keys on a tilted face is an error.
- **Parallel edges.** Edges that are parallel on the real object meet at the same vanishing point, or stay parallel together. Verticals stay vertical unless the whole view tilts.
- **Round parts.** Rims, wheels, lenses and holes are true ellipses whose narrowness matches the angle of their surface. A wheel seen edge-on beside a rim seen face-on is an error.
- **Contact.** The object meets its ground at the angle the ground is drawn.

Mild stylized perspective is fine. Report only what a careful viewer would see once it is pointed out.

Tools: a panel marks geometry measured on the enlarged object. Straight edges are coloured by their direction on the page, so edges of one colour are parallel in the picture. Black ellipses are curves that fit a true ellipse, with their width ratio and short axis: circles on one axle or one cylinder share that axis, and their ratios change gradually along it. Marks show where to look, not errors. Report only what you can see on the drawing.
