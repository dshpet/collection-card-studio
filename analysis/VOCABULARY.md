# Art vocabulary

Precise terms for describing a card. Each role receives its own section.

## Lighting and shading

- **Illumination:** Light incident on surfaces; distinguish its hypothesized organization from delivered colors.
- **Shading:** Image variation depicting form and light–surface interaction, including illustrated conventions.
- **Diffuse shading:** A component modeling diffuse illumination response under a declared decomposition; not every broad gradient.
- **Key:** Dominant organizing illumination role, not a direction or lamp type.
- **Fill:** Illumination opening shadows; not simply a brighter image background.
- **Negative fill:** Reduced surrounding bounce or fill, not emission of negative light.
- **Bounce/reflected fill:** Indirect light reflected from surrounding surfaces that contributes to illumination.
- **Backlight:** Illumination from behind the subject relative to the viewer.
- **Practical:** A depicted visible light source, not necessarily the dominant source.
- **Environment light:** Broad surrounding illumination, rather than one isolated accent.
- **Light direction:** Direction toward a hypothesized source in a declared frame.
- **Apparent source extent:** Angular size of a source as seen by a surface point.
- **Form/attached shadow:** Darker object surface turned away from illumination, not necessarily cast onto another surface.
- **Cast shadow:** Occlusion of illumination on a receiving surface.
- **Contact darkening:** Local darkening where surfaces meet or approach; supports grounding.
- **Umbra:** Region fully occluded from a specified extended light source.
- **Penumbra:** Region with partial visibility of an extended source.
- **Shadow softness:** Breadth of a shadow transition, separately from its darkness.
- **Specular highlight:** View-dependent reflected-light appearance, or its painted depiction.
- **Specular reflection:** Directionally concentrated reflection whose appearance depends on surface, illumination, and view.
- **Rim:** Illuminated-looking band near a silhouette, often associated with rear/side light.
- **Albedo:** Reflectance/base-color concept, separate from illumination in a declared model.

## Tone and value

- **Value:** Artistic light–dark placement or grouping; specify its numerical proxy separately.
- **Relative luminance Y:** Linear-light, reference-relative photometric coordinate derived from characterized RGB.
- **Brightness:** Perceived intensity of an area, affected by context.
- **Value grouping:** Organization into broad light, middle, and dark masses.
- **High/low key:** Predominantly light/dark appearance under an artistic convention.
- **Contrast:** Difference or variation between specified image values at a specified scale.
- **Roll-off:** How tonal values approach highlights or shadows, rather than an abrupt endpoint.
- **Clipping:** Values reaching an encoding/output bound after processing.
- **Chiaroscuro/tenebrism:** Prominent light–shade organization / illuminated details against a largely dark setting.

## Color and palette

- **Hue:** Chromatic direction, often a cyclic angle in a named space.
- **Chroma:** Colorfulness relative to an appropriate similarly illuminated reference.
- **Saturation:** Colorfulness relative to the area's own brightness; HSV S is one software coordinate.
- **Lightness:** Perceived brightness relative to an appropriate similarly illuminated white reference.
- **Palette:** Selected image colors and their masses/roles, not material albedo or a light spectrum.
- **Neutral share:** Fraction judged or defined as weakly chromatic in a named space.
- **Warm/cool relationship:** Contextual chromatic relationship between regions or accents.
- **Color difference:** Distance between specified color samples under a declared formula.
- **Gamut:** Colors representable by a specified encoding/device.

## Composition, hierarchy, and space

- **Framing:** Selection of artwork aperture, crop, and relation to canvas boundaries.
- **Occupancy:** Selected subject area divided by declared aperture area.
- **Margins:** Clearances between selected extent and aperture boundaries.
- **Negative space:** Space around/between positive forms; can be textured or active.
- **Balance:** Distribution of visual weight in a composition.
- **Hierarchy:** Ordering of perceptual/communicative importance.
- **Emphasis:** Means used to make a focal feature stand out.
- **Separation:** Distinguishability of subject from adjacent context.

## Shape and silhouette

- **Silhouette:** Projected outer shape with an explicit treatment of holes and attached parts.
- **Proportion:** Relative size of named parts or landmarks.
- **Aspect/elongation:** Width/height or principal-axis ratio, which must be distinguished.
- **Compactness:** Area–perimeter relationship under a declared formula.
- **Solidity:** Mask area divided by convex-hull area.
- **Openings/negative gaps:** Interior holes or spaces between diagnostic parts.
- **Apparent volume:** Perceived three-dimensional fullness.

## Edges, contours, and marks

- **Edge:** Local transition in image values, regardless of cause.
- **Contour:** Boundary describing shape or a selected region.
- **Outline:** Explicit contour-like graphic treatment around a form.
- **Stroke:** A mark with footprint, width, trajectory, and possibly taper.
- **Hard/soft edge:** Narrow/broad transition at a declared scale and contrast.
- **Lost edge:** Boundary with weak/absent visible separation in context.
- **Sfumato:** Gradual tonal modulation avoiding harsh outlines.

## Texture, markmaking, and material finish

- **Texture:** Spatial organization of repeated/local appearance variation.
- **Grain:** Fine granular variation; specify spatial scale and region.
- **Stipple:** Dot-like mark construction or its visible depiction.
- **Hatching:** Organized line marks encoding tone, form, or material.
- **Brush scale:** Mark size relative to native pixels or subject extent.
- **Mark direction:** Orientation field of visible marks.
- **Matte/glossy:** Weakly pronounced versus pronounced specular-looking appearance in context.
- **Metallic-looking:** Appearance suggesting metal through reflectance/color cues.
- **Translucent-looking:** Appearance suggesting light transport through/scattering within a material.

## Depth, viewpoint, and perspective

- **Occlusion/overlap:** One visible form covers another.
- **Projection:** Rule mapping geometry to an image, e.g. perspective or orthographic.
- **Perspective convergence:** Projected parallel directions meeting toward vanishing points.
- **Horizon:** Image line associated with a chosen world-horizontal plane at eye level.
- **Viewpoint:** Camera–object relationship determining visible faces.
- **Foreshortening:** Reduced projected extent along a receding direction.
- **Atmospheric perspective:** Distance-related appearance separation through scattering or its artistic depiction.
- **Blur/depth of field:** Image unsharpness / focus-related depth dependence.

## Rhythm and staging

- **Motif/pattern:** Recurring element / arrangement of repeated elements.
- **Pitch/gap:** Center-to-center repeat distance / boundary-to-boundary separation.
- **Alternation/progression:** Ordered category changes / systematic variation along a sequence.
- **Rhythm:** Organized recurrence and variation producing a sense of movement.
- **Staging:** Arrangement making an action or relation legible.
- **Gesture/expression:** Organized action cues / visible expressive configurations.
